Through chapter 6 of the Chāndogya, a father teaches his son with salt in water and bees in honey, and
nine times concludes: *tat tvam asi*, you are that. The repetition is the pedagogy; the sentence is
not information to store but a mirror to stand in front of, again and again.

The advaita tradition makes this the greatest of the four mahāvākyas (great sayings), and its
commentaries labour over every word, how "you" and "that" can possibly be one sentence. The gyan
path's whole discipline, viveka (discernment) and vairāgya (dispassion), exists to make the sentence
hearable rather than merely repeatable.
