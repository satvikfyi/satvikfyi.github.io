The tradition offers many purifiers, pilgrimage, ritual, austerity, repetition, and this verse
quietly ranks them. Jñāna is not information; it is seeing through (the root *jñā* is knowing as direct
acquaintance). What it purifies is the misidentification that every other impurity grows from.

Note the verse's small print: knowledge is found "in one's own self, in time"; not purchased, not
hurried. Gyan yoga is therefore less a technique than a long, honest looking, helped by study
(svādhyāya), reflection (manana) and the deep contemplation (nididhyāsana) the Vedānta texts prescribe.
