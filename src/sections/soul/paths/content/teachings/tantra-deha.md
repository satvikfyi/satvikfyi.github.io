The ascetic strand of Indian spirituality treats the body as a rope to be climbed and eventually
discarded; the tantric strand, astonishingly for its era, treats it as the temple where the liturgy
happens. Haṭha yoga itself descends from this conviction, the neti pots and breath counts of every
modern studio are distant descendants of tantric body-practice.

Both scholars cited here warn, usefully, against the two popular distortions; tantra as exotic sex
and tantra as mere self-help. In its own self-understanding it was a complete sādhanā: ritual,
mantra, body, and a metaphysics of śakti; transmitted under a teacher's eye. This page points at the
mountain; the climbing belongs to the lineages.
