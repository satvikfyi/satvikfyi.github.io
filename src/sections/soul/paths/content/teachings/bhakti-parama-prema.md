Nārada's little sūtra-collection does for devotion what Patañjali did for yoga, defines it, stages it,
defends it against its dilutions. The definition is precise: bhakti is *parama-prema*, supreme love,
and *ananyā*, admitting no rival. Later sūtras add its marks: it is not desire (it gives rather than
seeks), and the one who has it "wants nothing, grieves nothing, exults, and is ceaselessly devoted."

The verse-canon of bhakti, from the Āḻvārs to Mirabai to Tulsidas; is this definition sung out loud;
the sūtra is its prose form.
