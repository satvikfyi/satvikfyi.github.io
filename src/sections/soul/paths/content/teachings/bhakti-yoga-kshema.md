translation: "Those who worship me, thinking of none other, ever united with me; of them, whose devotion is constant, I carry the yoga-kṣema: I bring what they lack and preserve what they have."

The verse bhaktas lean on when they cannot lean on anything else. "Ananyāḥ", of no other; is the
whole qualification; no lineage, no learning, no worthiness list. In return, the divine takes over the
anxious half of life (acquiring, keeping) that so much of the mind's day is spent on.

Commentators note the modest grammar of the promise, *vahāmi*, "I carry", in the present tense, as a
habitual act. The tradition's reading: what the devotee releases is held. Vaiṣṇava homes have hung
this verse over doorways for centuries as a kind of quiet insurance policy of the heart.
