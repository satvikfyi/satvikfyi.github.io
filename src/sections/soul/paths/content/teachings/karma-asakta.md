Chapter 3 is the Gītā's karma-yoga chapter in the plain sense, work, duty, the ordinary life of a
householder, and this verse is its hinge. "The work that has to be done" (kāryaṁ karma) is
deliberately unglamorous: the filed return, the taught class, the tended field. What changes is the
grasping.

"Unattached" here translates asakta, without clinging, not without care. The tradition's image is
the lotus leaf: working in water, dry. Nothing in the verse licenses sloppiness; the fruit of
detachment is presence, and presence tends to make better work, not worse.
