Everyone knows the first line; the second is where the teaching bites. Kṛṣṇa does not say "abandon
results", results arrive whether wanted or not. He names the psychological error instead: letting
the imagined harvest write this morning's work. The cook cooking for applause is already a worse cook.

Note also what the verse forbids second: attachment to *inaction*. Nishkāma karma (desireless
action) is not withdrawal; it is work done whole, offered rather than traded. Gandhi ran an ashram, a
newspaper and a freedom movement on this half-verse, which is fair evidence that it does not mean
quietism.
