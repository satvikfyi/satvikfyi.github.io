---
name: Steady Gaze
slug: trataka
sanskritName: Trāṭaka
kind: practice
summary: A soft, unwavering gaze at one point; classically a candle flame, that gathers the eye's wandering into a single flame of attention.
tradition: haṭha yoga (a ṣaṭkarma); also yoga of the eyes in everyday teaching
effects:
  - Rapidly steadies a scattered mind through the steadiness of the eyes
  - Traditionally said to brighten the eyes and clear the "inner darkness"
  - An open-eyed alternative for those who drift or dull with closed eyes
  - A bridge toward visualization and inner-light practices
precautions:
  - Eye conditions, migraine or light sensitivity; use a low flame or a dark object instead
  - Never strain the gaze; blink when needed, tears are fine
  - Do not practise candle-gazing late at night if it energizes sleep away
steps:
  - Place a candle at arm's length, flame at eye height; room dim.
  - Sit comfortably; soften the gaze onto the flame, the whole flame, not a point.
  - Keep watching without blinking as long as comfortable; blink when you must.
  - When the eyes tire, close them and hold the after-image at the eyebrow centre.
  - Keep the inner flame as long as it fades; then open the eyes and rest.
durationGuidance: 5–10 minutes; a classic evening practice.
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 2, verse 31–32; trāṭaka among the cleansings
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

The Pradīpikā files trāṭaka under the six cleansings, not under meditation; because the attention it
cleanses is the drifter's attention. Modern eyes, raised on flickering screens, find it both harder and
more profitable than any earlier generation did.
