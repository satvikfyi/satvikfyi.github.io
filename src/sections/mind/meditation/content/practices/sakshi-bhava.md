---
name: The Witness Attitude
slug: sakshi-bhava
sanskritName: Sākṣī-Bhāva
kind: concept
summary: The understanding that you are the watcher of experience, not the experience; an attitude that slowly reorganizes everything.
tradition: advaita vedānta; adopted by the tantric Witness practices
effects:
  - Creates standing distance from moods, thoughts and reputations
  - The traditional medicine for over-identification with the drama of the day
  - Ripens, in advaita, into the inquiry "who is the witness?"
  - Works inside every practice, asana, breath, work
precautions:
  - Witnessing must not become dissociation, feel fully, watch what feels
  - The inquiry can destabilize a fragile sense of self; hold it lightly or with a teacher
steps:
  - In any sitting, ask once, gently; "to whom is this appearing?", and rest before any answer.
  - Notice that everything observed, body, breath, thought, mood; is, by that fact, not the observer.
  - Do not try to observe the observer; the observing is the pointing, not a thing to catch.
  - Carry the attitude into one charged moment a day, watch the irritation instead of being it.
durationGuidance: An attitude, folded into every practice; formal inquiry 10 minutes when stable.
sources:
  - title: Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad
    author: Yājñavalkya's teachings
    reference: 3.7.23, "he is the unseen seer, the unheard hearer… the witness of all"
  - title: Dr̥g-dr̥śya-viveka
    author: attributed to Śaṅkara's circle
    reference: the seer–seen discrimination as method
---

The Upaniṣad's phrase is exact, the witness is "the unseen seer, the unheard hearer, the unthought
thinker". Sākṣī-bhāva is simply standing where that phrase points, as often as remembered. It costs no
extra minute; it borrows them from ones already being lived.
