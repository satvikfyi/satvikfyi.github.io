---
name: Open, Choiceless Awareness
slug: choiceless-awareness
kind: practice
summary: Attention with no object at all, whatever arises (sound, thought, itch, joy) is noticed and let pass, choosing nothing.
tradition: vipassanā's later stages; the zazen of neighbours; advaita's abidance
effects:
  - Dissolves the habit of picking and pursuing that runs ordinary attention
  - Reveals the changeless watching underneath the changing watched
  - The mature fruit of concentration practice, not a substitute for it
  - Traditionally the doorway to effortless being
precautions:
  - Build concentration first (weeks of ānāpāna at least), otherwise this becomes daydreaming
  - Dullness and drift are the failure modes; wakefulness first, openness second
  - Not recommended as a sole practice during acute distress
steps:
  - Sit as usual; take a few minutes with the breath to collect attention.
  - Then drop the breath as an object, simply be aware of whatever is presenting.
  - Sounds arrive and leave; thoughts arrive and leave; nothing is followed, nothing pushed away.
  - When you notice you have drifted into a story, return gently to open watching.
  - End the sitting deliberately; carry the openness into one ordinary activity.
durationGuidance: 10–20 minutes after grounding; grows with stability.
sources:
  - title: Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta's ripening
    author: the Buddha (Majjhima Nikāya 10)
    reference: observation without grasping, the root instruction
  - title: Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali
    author: Patañjali
    reference: 1.41, the jewel-like settled mind reflecting whatever stands before it
---

Every tradition arrives here eventually, wearing different clothes; the Buddha's choiceless notice,
Patañjali's transparent jewel, the zazen of "just sitting". The instruction is almost no instruction,
which is why teachers insist on the concentration practices first: openness without stability is
merely vagueness with good intentions.
