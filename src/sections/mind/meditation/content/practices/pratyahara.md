---
name: "Pratyāhāra: The Withdrawal"
slug: pratyahara
sanskritName: Pratyāhāra
kind: concept
summary: The fifth limb of yoga, the senses, drawn back from their objects like a turtle's limbs into its shell; the hinge between outer and inner practice.
tradition: Pātañjala yoga
effects:
  - Explains why meditation stalls when the senses stay wired outward
  - The bridge limb, after it, practice moves from posture and breath to mind itself
  - Reframes rest itself as a practice, not an absence
  - Prepares dhāraṇā; without it, concentration fights the open windows
precautions:
  - Withdrawal is not suppression, the senses quiet because they are fed inward, not starved by force
  - Do not attempt "shutting the senses" in traffic or while operating anything
steps:
  - At day's end, sit; list inwardly the five senses and where each is still hooked outward.
  - One by one, let each settle; eyes closed, sounds allowed to be background, tongue resting.
  - Notice the effort is not in pulling but in no longer reaching, like a hand unclenching.
  - Sit in the gathered quiet a few minutes; this gatheredness is the limb.
  - Read Yoga Sūtra 2.54–55 and feel its promise, then the senses imitate the settled mind.
durationGuidance: A concept to practise at dusk, and whenever the day has been loud.
sources:
  - title: Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali
    author: Patañjali
    reference: 2.54, withdrawal is the senses' non-contact with their objects, as if imitating the mind
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: the turtle image, echoed in the haṭha corpus
---

Patañjali devotes two sūtras to pratyāhāra and centuries of practitioners to understanding them. The
turtle image, limbs drawn in, not amputated; is the whole caution and the whole instruction: the
senses withdraw because the mind stopped dispatching them, not because they were gagged.
