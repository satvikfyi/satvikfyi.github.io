---
name: Yogic Sleep
slug: yoga-nidra
sanskritName: Yoga Nidrā
kind: practice
summary: Profound systematic relaxation while lying awake, the body sleeps, the awareness watches; rest deeper than sleep's, it is said.
tradition: tantric origin; systematized in the modern Bihar school
effects:
  - Deep rest, traditionally rated above sleep for recuperation
  - Releases accumulated tension the waking mind never notices
  - A seed-bed for intention (saṅkalpa) planted in a receptive mind
  - Accessible to almost everyone, including the ill and the exhausted
precautions:
  - Can surface strong emotion or memory, go gently, stay with the voice of the guide
  - Actual sleep is a common and forgivable failure; repeat, or accept the nap
  - Dissociative tendencies, practise with a teacher's guidance
steps:
  - Lie on the back in śavāsana, arms away from the body, eyes closed; cover yourself if cool.
  - Set a short, positive intention (saṅkalpa) and repeat it three times.
  - Follow the guided rotation of awareness through the whole body, part by part, without moving.
  - Rest in the awareness of breath, of sensations, of pairs of opposites; as guided.
  - Return slowly, breath, intention, gentle movement, and roll to the side before rising.
durationGuidance: 20–40 minutes, guided (recordings abound); any hour, ideally not right after food.
sources:
  - title: Yoga Nidrā
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
    reference: the Bihar school's systematic method
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: the precursors, nyāsa and śavāsana in the tradition
---

Yoga nidrā means "yogic sleep", and the paradox is exact: the body descends toward sleep while a thin
thread of awareness stays lit. The tantric technique of nyāsa, placing awareness through the body; 
is its ancestor; Satyananda gave it the form now taught in hospitals and ashrams alike.
