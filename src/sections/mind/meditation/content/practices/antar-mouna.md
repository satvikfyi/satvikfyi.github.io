---
name: Inner Silence
slug: antar-mouna
sanskritName: Antara Mauna
kind: practice
summary: Watch the mind's noise on purpose, thoughts, sensations, moods; until, seen and un-fed, it quiets by itself.
tradition: tantric (Bihar school systematization of older vīkācana methods)
effects:
  - Turns the restless mind itself into the object of meditation
  - Weakens identification with thoughts, they become weather, not self
  - Progressive and self-paced; each stage earns the next
  - A traditional door to witness-consciousness (sākṣī-bhāva)
precautions:
  - Not a first practice, sit with the breath for some months first
  - Trauma histories or acute anxiety, practise with a teacher's guidance
  - "Seeing thoughts must not become arguing with them, return to watching"
steps:
  - Sit as for meditation; let the mind do exactly as it likes, and watch.
  - Categorize gently, sound, sensation, thought, memory; without following each one out.
  - When a thought hooks you, notice the hooking; that noticing is the stage being learned.
  - Later stages, a single chosen thought held, then released; then no-thought at all.
  - End by resting in whatever quiet remains; it deepens over years, not days.
durationGuidance: 15–30 minutes, after some grounding in breath practice.
sources:
  - title: Yoga Nidrā & the mauna teachings
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
    reference: antara mauna staged in the Bihar tradition
  - title: Vichāra-sūtra lineage methods
    author: tantric vīkācana (thought-observation) practices
---

Antara mauna inverts the usual instruction: instead of quieting the mind to meditate, you meditate on
the mind's noise until the noise, thoroughly seen and thoroughly un-fed, runs out of material. It is
patient work, the tantric traditions that shaped it were nothing if not patient.
