---
name: Walking Meditation
slug: walking-meditation
sanskritName: Caṅkama
kind: practice
summary: Meditation at walking pace, the foot's rise, move and place; for minds that go stirry the moment they sit still.
tradition: Buddhist caṅkama; the walking passages of Indian monasteries
effects:
  - A workable door for restless (vāta-type) minds that sitting defeats
  - Links meditation into ordinary movement, streets, corridors, gardens
  - Aids digestion and settles the body after long sitting
  - Traditionally the companion of sitting, not its replacement
precautions:
  - Practise where the path is safe and level; eyes stay open
  - Traffic and headphones do not mix with this practice
steps:
  - Find a quiet lane or a 5–10 metre stretch you can walk back and forth.
  - Stand still a moment; feel the whole body standing.
  - Walk noticeably slower than normal; with each step know, lifting, moving, placing.
  - At the lane's end, stop fully; turn with awareness; continue.
  - Attention on the feet soles and the ankles' work, speed can stay near-normal outdoors.
durationGuidance: 15–20 minutes; beautiful between sitting sessions.
sources:
  - title: Caṅkama, the walking practice
    author: the Buddha's monastic discipline (Vinaya / commentaries)
    reference: the walking paths (caṅkamana) of the early monasteries
  - title: The Heart of Yoga
    author: T. K. V. Desikachar
---

Every old Indian monastery grew worn walking paths, the caṅkamana; for exactly the minds that today
pace their flats between meetings. The practice concedes nothing to sitting: many teachers say the
Buddha himself walked into his deepest hours.
