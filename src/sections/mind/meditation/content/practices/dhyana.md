effects:
  - Names precisely what all the practices in this module aim at
  - Distinguishes concentration (dhāraṇā), meditation (dhyāna) and absorption (samādhi)
  - Prevents the common confusion of relaxation with meditation
  - Gives the tradition's measure of progress, continuity, not fireworks
precautions:
  - Reading about dhyāna is not dhyāna; this entry maps the territory, the practices walk it
steps:
  - Compare the three rungs, dhāraṇā is attention returned again and again; dhyāna is attention that no longer leaves; samādhi is attention in which only the object remains.
  - Notice in your own sitting where you spend most minutes, that is dhāraṇā, and it is the honest stage.
  - Watch for the moments the returning stops being needed, brief, un-dramatic continuity; that taste is dhyāna.
  - Read Yoga Sūtra 3.2 alongside a sitting practice, not instead of one.

Sūtra 3.2 is one of the shortest definitions in world literature, tatra pratyayaika-tānatā dhyānam:
there, the single-minded flow of the same idea is meditation. The image the commentaries love is poured
oil, one unbroken stream, not beads. Everything on this site marked "practice" is a device for
reaching that flow; this entry is only its name.
