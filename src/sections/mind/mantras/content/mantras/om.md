---
name: "Oṁ: the Praṇava"
slug: om
deity: The unmanifest, source of all sound
tradition: Vedic, the root of every later tradition
category: foundation
summary: The one syllable before all words, the sound of the Upaniṣads' silence, chanted at the beginning and end of nearly everything.
text: ॐ
transliteration: "Oṁ (A-U-M)"
meaning: "All this is the syllable Oṁ. Its parts, a, u, m; are the waking, dreaming and sleeping states; the silence after is the fourth, the Self (the Māṇḍūkya's whole teaching)."
meter: Bīja (seed syllable), one unbroken tone
usage: "Chanted at the start and close of practice, study and prayer; held at the end until it dissolves into the silence it points to. Traditionally sounded as three and a half mātrās, a, u, m, and the silence."
sources:
  - title: Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad
    author: the Upaniṣadic seer
    reference: all twelve verses unfold Oṁ as the whole of experience
  - title: Bhagavad Gītā
    author: Vyāsa (traditional attribution)
    reference: 8.13, uttering the single syllable Oṁ at the end, one attains the highest goal
---

Every mantra on this page is, in the tradition's understanding, an extension of this one. The
Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad spends all twelve of its verses on it and still ends by pointing past the sound to
the quiet after, which is why teachers end the chanting there, and wait.
