name: "So'ham: the Breath's Own Mantra"

deity: The Self (as the breath's quiet self-statement)
tradition: Advaita Vedānta; tantric & haṭha adoption
category: peace
summary: The mantra no one gave you, the breath saying "I am That" all day, unheard, waiting to be noticed.
text: सोऽहम्
transliteration: "so'ham (saḥ + aham, \"that I am\")"
meaning: "The inhale sounds as 'so', the exhale as 'ham'; together, \"I am That\", the Upaniṣadic mahāvākya repeated by the body twenty thousand times a day."
meter: Ajapa-japa, repetition without repeating (the breath does it)
usage: "Not chanted aloud but listened for: sit quietly, let the breath grow quiet, and hear the subtle so on the in-breath, ham on the out. The haṭha texts call this ajapa-gāyatrī, the unchanted chant, already going."

The tradition's quietest joke: you have been repeating the greatest of statements since your first
cry, and calling it breathing. So'ham practice is only listening in. Vedānta's grand declaration and
the body's ordinary rhythm turn out to be the same sentence.
