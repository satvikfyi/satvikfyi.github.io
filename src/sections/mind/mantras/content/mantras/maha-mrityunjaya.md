deity: Śiva as Tryambaka, the three-eyed one
tradition: Vedic (Ṛgveda, later Śaiva devotion)
category: protection
summary: The great "conqueror of death", chanted for healing, for strength in illness, and for the ripening that lets go of fear.
text: ॐ त्र्यम्बकं यजामहे सुगन्धिं पुष्टिवर्धनम् उर्वारुकमिव बन्धनान् मृत्योर्मुक्षीय मामृतात्
transliteration: "oṁ tryambakaṁ yajāmahe sugandhiṁ puṣṭi-vardhanam urvārukam iva bandhanān mṛtyor mukṣīya māmṛtāt"
meaning: "We worship the three-eyed One, fragrant, the nourisher of all; as the ripe cucumber is released from its stem, so may I be released from death, not from immortality."
meter: Gāyatrī (a later variant), 24 syllables × 3
usage: "Chanted 108 times in times of illness, before surgery, on behalf of the ailing, and in the Mahāmṛtyuñjaya japa traditions; also sung at Śivarātri. Its cucumber image, release by ripeness, not by wrenching; carries the mantra's whole spirit."

The image at its heart is agricultural and exact: the ripe cucumber leaves the vine of its own
readiness. The tradition reads death itself that way, fear of it lessens as one ripens, and the
mantra is chanted at bedsides for exactly that double comfort: healing if healing comes, ripeness
either way.
