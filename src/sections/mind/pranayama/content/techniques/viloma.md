---
name: Interrupted Breath
slug: viloma
sanskritName: Viloma Prāṇāyāma
category: balancing
level: beginner
summary: Pause the inhale, sip, sip, sip; then pour the exhale out the same way; a gentle ladder that lengthens the breath without force.
benefits:
  - Extends lung capacity gradually, without the strain of direct stretching
  - Trains fine control and patience in the breath
  - Calming and focusing; useful before meditation
  - Accessible to beginners and safe for most recovering bodies
contraindications:
  - Respiratory infection or severe asthma, wait or keep pauses minimal
  - If any pause creates air hunger or anxiety, return to continuous breathing
  - Pregnancy, practise the gentle, hold-free version only
steps:
  - Lie or sit comfortably; breathe normally once.
  - Inhale in three sips, a short draw, a pause, another, a pause; filling gradually to the top.
  - Exhale in one smooth, slow release.
  - For the second stage, inhale in one smooth draw, then exhale in three unhurried pours.
  - 5–6 rounds of each stage.
rhythm: Sipped inhale, smooth exhale; then smooth inhale, poured exhale.
durationGuidance: 5–6 rounds per stage, at any quiet hour.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
    reference: the interrupted breath as preparatory prāṇāyāma
  - title: The Heart of Yoga
    author: T. K. V. Desikachar
---

Vi-loma means "against the hair", against the grain of the ordinary breath. Where forceful systems
demand length immediately, this one builds it the way one carries water up a stair: in easy,
spill-proof steps. It is Desikachar's kind of practice, engineered for the actual human arriving at it.
