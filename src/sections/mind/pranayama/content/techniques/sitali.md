---
name: Cooling Breath
slug: sitali
sanskritName: Śītalī Prāṇāyāma
category: cooling
level: intermediate
summary: Curl the tongue and draw the breath across it, the haṭha tradition's built-in air conditioner for hot heads and hot summers.
benefits:
  - Cools the body and cools tempers, the classic pitta remedy
  - Reduces thirst and the dry, hot feeling of overwork
  - Traditionally said to reduce hunger pangs and fevers (mild ones)
  - Refreshes the mind in heat where deep breathing feels impossible
contraindications:
  - Avoid in cold weather or a chilled body, it cools the system further
  - Do not practise with a chest infection or heavy congestion
  - Vāta and kapha constitutions should use it sparingly, in summer only
steps:
  - Sit comfortably; curl the tongue lengthwise into a tube (genetic ability varies, see Sītkarī if you cannot).
  - Protrude the curled tongue slightly between the lips.
  - Inhale slowly through the tongue-tube, the air arrives cool and moist.
  - Draw the tongue in; close the mouth; hold the breath a moment.
  - Exhale slowly through the nose. 6–10 rounds.
rhythm: Cool inhale through the tongue; brief comfortable hold; slow nose exhale.
durationGuidance: 6–10 rounds in heat or after heated effort; seasonal use.
associatedDoshas:
  - pitta
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 2, verses 54–55; the cooling breaths that vanquish heat
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

The Pradīpikā lists the cooling breaths among the practices that conquer "the great heat", a promise
the Indian summer makes literal. For pitta constitutions, summer workers, and anyone whose temper runs
the thermostat up, this is the tradition's most direct coolant.
