---
name: Skull-Shining Breath
slug: kapalabhati
sanskritName: Kapālabhāti
category: cleansing
level: intermediate
summary: Sharp, active exhales that pump the belly and scrub the fog out; the morning cleanse of the haṭha tradition.
benefits:
  - Clears stale air and mucus from the lower lungs
  - Wakes up the mind, the traditional antidote to kapha dullness
  - Tones the abdominal muscles and diaphragm
  - Warmth and clarity on sluggish mornings
contraindications:
  - Pregnancy, high blood pressure, heart conditions; avoid
  - During menstruation or recent abdominal surgery, avoid
  - Epilepsy, panic disorder or vertigo; avoid without professional guidance
  - Never strain, stop at the first sign of dizziness or ringing ears
steps:
  - Sit tall with the spine long; hands on the knees or belly.
  - Take one easy inhale to begin.
  - Exhale sharply by snapping the navel back toward the spine; let the inhale fall in on its own.
  - One pump per second to start, 20 strokes is a full round.
  - After the final exhale, sit still and feel the skull "shine"; breathe normally, then repeat once or twice.
rhythm: "Active exhale, passive inhale; roughly 1 stroke per second; 20 strokes per round, 1–3 rounds"
durationGuidance: 1–3 rounds of 20 strokes on an empty stomach, mornings only.
associatedDoshas:
  - kapha
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 2, verses 21–22; kapālabhāti among the six cleansings (ṣaṭkarma)
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

Kapālabhāti sits in the Pradīpikā among the ṣaṭkarmas, the six purifications; not among the
prāṇāyāmas proper: it is a cleansing whose side effect is clarity. The name means "skull-shining", and
one honest round on a foggy morning explains it better than any commentary.
