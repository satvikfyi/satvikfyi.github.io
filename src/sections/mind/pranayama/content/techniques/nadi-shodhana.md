---
name: Alternate Nostril Breathing
slug: nadi-shodhana
sanskritName: Nāḍī Śodhana Prāṇāyāma
category: balancing
level: beginner
summary: The queen of prāṇāyāmas, alternate breathing through the left and right nostril to purify the channels and settle the whole system.
benefits:
  - Purifies and balances the nāḍīs (the energy channels of the subtle body)
  - Balances the two hemispheres of thought, steady focus, calm alertness
  - Tridoshic by tradition, settles vāta, cools pitta, stirs kapha
  - An excellent daily base practice and preparation for meditation
contraindications:
  - Do not force retention (kumbhaka) in the early stages, practise without holds first
  - If a nostril is blocked, practise on the flowing side only, or wait
  - Fever, acute cold or ear infection; rest instead
steps:
  - Sit comfortably tall with the spine long; rest the left hand on the knee.
  - Fold the index and middle fingers of the right hand; the thumb guards the right nostril, the ring finger the left.
  - Close the right nostril and exhale fully through the left.
  - Inhale slowly through the left; close it; open the right and exhale; inhale through the right.
  - That is one round, continue smooth, quiet rounds for 5–10 minutes, ending with an exhale on the left.
rhythm: "Begin 1:0:1 (inhale and exhale equal, no hold); with training, the classical 1:4:2 may be approached"
durationGuidance: 5–10 minutes daily; 5–9 rounds are plenty to begin.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
  - pitta
  - kapha
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 2, verses 7–10; purifying the nāḍīs before retention practice
  - title: Light on Pranayama
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
---

Śodhana means purification: the practice is named for what it does to the nāḍīs, the channels through
which haṭha yoga understands prāṇa to move. The Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā insists the channels be purified
before any breath retention is attempted, the gentle alternation is that purification, done slowly,
never noisily.
