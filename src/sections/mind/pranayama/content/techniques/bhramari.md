---
name: Bee Breath
slug: bhramari
sanskritName: Bhramarī Prāṇāyāma
category: calming
level: beginner
summary: The humming bee breath, a low hum on the exhale that vibrates the head quiet; the traditional antidote to a loud mind.
benefits:
  - Rapidly calms anxiety, anger and mental noise
  - Gently vibrates and relaxes the face, jaw and skull
  - Traditional support for insomnia when practised before bed
  - Safe for nearly everyone, at any age
contraindications:
  - Ear infection or acute ear pain, avoid humming
  - Keep the mouth closed gently, the jaw unclenched
  - If dizziness arises, return to ordinary breathing
steps:
  - Sit comfortably; let the eyes close and the jaw soften.
  - Optionally cover the ears gently with the thumbs or palms (liśed bhramarī).
  - Inhale softly through the nose.
  - Exhale with a low, even hum; feel the skull buzz like a bee's drone.
  - 6–10 rounds, then sit in the silence the hum leaves behind.
rhythm: Natural inhale; the hum lengthens the exhale without counting.
durationGuidance: 6–10 rounds at any time of day; lovely before sleep.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
  - pitta
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 2, verse 68; the bee-sound practice leading to samādhi's bliss
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

The Pradīpikā promises the yogi who fills the mind with the drone of the bee "yogic bliss", hyperbole
until you try it on a noisy evening. The hum gives the busy mind a single, bodily sensation to fold
into, and the quiet after the last round is the practice's real fruit.
