---
name: Three-Part Breath
slug: dirga
sanskritName: Dīrgha Prāṇāyāma
category: calming
level: beginner
summary: The full yogic breath, belly, ribs, chest; the foundational practice that teaches where the breath actually goes.
benefits:
  - Trains complete use of the lungs, gently and without strain
  - Grounds and steadies, the classic first remedy for anxious, high breathing
  - Prepares the body-mind for every other prāṇāyāma
  - Can be practised lying down, making it ideal for beginners and illness recovery
contraindications:
  - Never inflate to the point of tension, full, not stuffed
  - Respiratory illness, keep the pace gentle and pause when needed
steps:
  - Lie down or sit; rest the hands on the belly.
  - Inhale into the belly, let it rise like a balloon.
  - Continue the same inhale into the ribs, then the upper chest.
  - Exhale in reverse, chest, ribs, belly sinking.
  - Ten slow, wave-like rounds; finish with normal breathing.
rhythm: Smooth and continuous with no holds; simply full.
durationGuidance: 10 rounds or 3–5 minutes; a daily foundation practice.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
    reference: the full yogic breath as the base practice
  - title: Light on Pranayama
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
---

Before any counting, any retention, any technique; there is simply learning to breathe wholly. Dīrgha
(the "long" breath) is that lesson: most of us breathe into the top third of the lungs and call it
living, and this practice quietly corrects the habit.
