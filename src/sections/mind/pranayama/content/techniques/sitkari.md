---
name: Hissing Breath
slug: sitkari
sanskritName: Sītkarī Prāṇāyāma
category: cooling
level: intermediate
summary: The cooling breath for everyone, teeth together, lips parted, air hissing in; the sister practice Śītalī was named beside.
benefits:
  - Cools body and mind, exactly like Śītalī, but available to every mouth
  - Refreshes in heat, after exertion, in anger's first flush
  - Traditionally held to preserve beauty of face and freshness (haṭha tradition's own claim)
  - Settles pitta agitation quickly
contraindications:
  - Avoid in cold weather or when chilled
  - Sensitive teeth or jaw tension, keep the bite gentle
  - Congestion or respiratory infection, wait
steps:
  - Sit comfortably; press the teeth lightly together and part the lips.
  - Inhale slowly through the teeth, a soft hiss of cool air.
  - Close the mouth and hold the breath a moment, feeling the coolness land.
  - Exhale slowly through the nose.
  - 6–10 rounds; finish with a few normal breaths.
rhythm: Hissing inhale through the teeth; brief hold; slow nose exhale.
durationGuidance: 6–10 rounds when heat, inner or outer; is high.
associatedDoshas:
  - pitta
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 2, verses 54–55; named beside Śītalī among the cooling breaths
  - title: Light on Pranayama
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
---

Where Śītalī needs a curled tongue that genetics denies half of humanity, Sītkarī asks only for
teeth and lips. The haṭha texts present them as a pair; choose the one your mouth was built for.
