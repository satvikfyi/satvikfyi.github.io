---
name: Equal Breathing
slug: sama-vritti
sanskritName: Sama-vṛtti Prāṇāyāma
category: balancing
level: beginner
summary: Make inhale and exhale the same quiet length, the simplest possible practice, and one of the most quietly profound.
benefits:
  - Balances the breath and, with it, attention
  - Builds concentration on an easy, constant count
  - Settles vāta's unevenness and pitta's rush in the same sitting
  - A discreet practice, usable anywhere, on a bus or before a meeting
contraindications:
  - If the count creates strain, shorten it; equality matters, length does not
  - Skip breath retention until trained (keep it sama-vṛtti, not box breathing, at first)
steps:
  - Sit tall; breathe normally for a moment and notice the breath's rhythm.
  - Count the inhale silently, one, two, three, four.
  - Exhale to the same count.
  - Keep both equally comfortable; adjust the number down if needed.
  - Continue for 5 minutes; let the counting fade into the rhythm.
rhythm: 1:0:1, equal inhale and exhale (4 or 5 counts is a common start).
durationGuidance: 5 minutes, once or twice daily.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
  - pitta
sources:
  - title: Light on Pranayama
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: equal ration as the foundation of rhythm training
  - title: The Heart of Yoga
    author: T. K. V. Desikachar
---

Sama means equal; vṛtti means movement or wave. The instruction is the whole practice in two words, 
make the two waves of the breath the same size. Desikachar's lineage treats it as the patient gateway
to every more elaborate ratio.
