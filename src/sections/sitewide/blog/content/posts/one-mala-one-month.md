Of everything on this site, japa; the repetition of a mantra, asks the least and gives the
strangest. This is a short report from a month of it: one mala, 108 beads, one round each morning,
the same name every time.

## The setup

The rules were the traditional ones. Same time (before the phone wakes up), same seat, the beads
moved with the thumb and middle finger, the breath allowed to find the rhythm of the name rather
than the other way around. The mantra itself does not matter for the experiment, 
[pick one you can love](/mind/mantras/), but consistency does: the tradition is blunt that one
mantra for a long time outperforms many mantras for a short time.

## Week one: the inventory

The first week is a tour of one's own restlessness. The mind produces, on average, one urgent
memory, two imaginary arguments, and a song lyric per nine beads. The instruction from every japa
text is the same: do not fight, do not indulge; *return*. The mala keeps score so you don't have
to.

## Week two: the slowing

Somewhere in the second week the repetitions stop feeling like counting and start feeling like
swinging. The beads' pace sets itself; the mantra begins repeating *itself* in the gaps, the
tradition calls this ajapa, the unchanted chant, and it turns out the breath has been murmuring
something all along.

## Weeks three and four: the spill

The quiet effect of the month is not on the seat; it is in the day. The name surfaces at red
lights, before difficult emails, in the produce aisle. Nothing mystical is claimed, only that
attention, given a home, visits it uninvited. By day thirty the practice takes eleven minutes and
feels like the keel of the morning.

## Verdict

The [meditation pages](/mind/meditation/) teach the mechanics of japa; this is the field report.
One mala, one month: the cheapest experiment on this site, and the one we would repeat first.
