New visitors sometimes write in to ask why the site spells it *Tāḍāsana* and not *Tadasana*, or
*prāṇāyāma* instead of *pranayama*. The dots and lines look fussy. They are not decoration, they
are pronunciation instructions, and dropping them quietly changes the words.

## What the marks do

- **The macron (ā, ī, ū)** doubles a vowel's length. *Tāḍāsana* has four long syllables; saying
  "ta-DA-sa-na" with a short first *a* is a different rhythm than the word owns.
- **The dot under (ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, ṣ)** means the tongue curls back to the roof of the mouth. English
  barely has these sounds; the dot is the difference between *śāsana* (teaching) and *ṣaṣa*
  (rabbit) territory.
- **The ś vs ṣ** distinction, the hiss with the tongue forward versus curled; is exactly the one
  Sanskrit grammarians named first among the sounds.
- **The ṁ (anusvāra)** is the resonant nose-hum in *Oṁ*, not an English "m" clipped short.

This system is called **IAST** (International Alphabet of Sanskrit Transliteration), and it has been
the scholarly standard for over a century, it is how the texts themselves are printed in the
editions we cite.

## Why it matters here

The mission of this site is to be [source-first](/about/): when we tell you the Haṭha Yoga
Pradīpikā says *sthira-sukham āsanam*, we would like you to be able to sound it out exactly as a
pandit would, not approximately as an airport signboard might. Every [mantra](/mind/mantras/) on
the site carries its full IAST transliteration for the same reason, a mantra is, among other
things, a precise arrangement of sounds.

## And when you just want to type it

We get it. Search boxes and URLs stay simple on purpose, `/mind/pranayama/` is welcome to stay
dotless. Precision lives in the text; convenience lives in the address bar.
