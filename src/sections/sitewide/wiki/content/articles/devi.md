The Goddess is not one deity but a horizon: Sarasvatī with her veena (learning), Lakṣmī on her
lotus (fortune), Pārvatī on the mountain (devotion as marriage), Durgā on her lion (unconquerable
grace), Kālī with her sword (time itself). The Śākta traditions revere her as the supreme, Śakti,
the power without which even the gods are still.

## The Devī Māhātmya

The fifth-century hymn of her glories is recited across the subcontinent during Navarātri, nine
nights each autumn. Its refrain, *yā devī sarva-bhūteṣu*, "she who abides in all beings as
power", states the theology entire; this site's [tantra pages](/soul/tantra/) touch its Śākta
afterlife.

## In the household

Her worship is the daily [pūjā](/wiki/worship/puja/), the lamps of [āratī](/wiki/worship/arati/),
the sarasvatī-invocation sung before every Indian concert.
