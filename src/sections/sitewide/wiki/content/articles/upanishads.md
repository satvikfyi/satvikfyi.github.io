The Upaniṣads (the name suggests "sitting near", the student at the teacher's side) are the
concluding, philosophical portions of the Vedas, composed over centuries from roughly 800 BCE. They
are not treatises but transcripts: kings questioning sages, sons teaching fathers, a woman
(Gārgī) holding her own in debate.

## The core discoveries

Behind the world's variety lies one reality (*Brahman*); behind the individual's shifting
experience, one self (*Ātman*), and these two, the texts keep concluding, are identical; *tat
tvam asi*, "you are that". Liberation ([mokṣa](/wiki/concepts/moksha/)) is not earned but
recognized.

## Where to start

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Chāndogya (the oldest, longest); the Kaṭha (the boy Naciketās and death
itself); the Māṇḍūkya (twelve verses on the syllable Oṁ). Easwaran's translation reads like what
they are, conversations.
