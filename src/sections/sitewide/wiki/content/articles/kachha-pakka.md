Before refrigeration, Indian food culture organized itself around a practical split:
**kachha** dishes cooked in water — dals, boiled vegetables, plain rice — perishable and
eaten within the day; **pakka** dishes cooked in ghee or fried — puris, laddus, khaja —
stable enough to carry, share and send.

Puri's temple formalises it as sankhudi (the wet abhada of the day) against nisankhudi (the
dry sweets pilgrims carry home). The divide is why prasadam that travels is ghee-and-sugar
by design, and why the everyday meal pot and the offering pot answer to different clocks.
Recipes on this site note which side they live on.
