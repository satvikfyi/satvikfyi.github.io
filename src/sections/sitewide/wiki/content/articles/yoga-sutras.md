Composed around the first centuries CE, the Yoga Sūtras gathered a millennium of practice into 195
telegraphic aphorisms. Patañjali defines yoga in the second sūtra, *yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*,
the stilling of the mind's turnings, and then lays out the technology: the eight limbs from ethics
to absorption that this site's [ashtanga pages](/soul/meditation/) map in full.

## How it reads

A sūtra is a thread, the minimum words needed to hold a teaching, meant to be unpacked by a
teacher. "Yoga is restraint of the modifications of the mind-stuff" (Isherwood's rendering of 1.2)
has seeded a thousand commentaries, beginning with Vyāsa's in the fifth century.

## Why it matters here

Posture is thread three of eight; breath is thread four. The modern studio's inheritance is real
but partial, and the Sūtras are where the whole architecture lives.
