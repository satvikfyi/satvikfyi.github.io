Steaming is the quiet technique of the temple kitchen: nothing burns, nothing browns,
and the food stays soft enough for offering and sharing. The idli is its monument —
[fermented batter](/wiki/cooking-techniques/fermenting-batters/) steamed in plates or, in the
older way, in moulded cups.

Older still is the leaf: kotte kadubu steamed in jackfruit-leaf cones, Kanchipuram's idli
once in mandharai-leaf baskets — the leaf seasons as the steam works. Steaming also sets
dhokla, patra and the modaks of Ganesh Chaturthi. Wet heat keeps the Kachha character of
the food — fresh-eaten, not kept.
