Sabudana is pearl tapioca — starch from cassava roots, a crop that reached India only
in the colonial era, yet within a few generations became *the* [fasting](/wiki/cooking-techniques/phalahari-cooking/) starch of the
Deccan and the North. Sabudana khichdi, vada and kheer define Ekadashi and Navratri tables.

The technique is everything: wash, then soak just covered for hours, until a pearl squeezed
between fingers gives fully without a hard core. Too little water and the centre stays
stone; too much and it sludges. Cooked right, the pearls turn glassy and separate.
