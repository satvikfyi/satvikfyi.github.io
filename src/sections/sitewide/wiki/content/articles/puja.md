Pūjā is hospitality made sacred. The image (mūrti) is woken, bathed, dressed, fed and honoured
exactly as one would honour a guest who matters most, because in the tradition's understanding,
the divine consents to be present in the form for the sake of the devotee's heart.

## The steps

A simplified daily household pūjā: invitation (āvāhana), seat and water, bath and garments,
sandalpaste and flowers, incense, lamp ([āratī](/wiki/worship/arati/)), food offering, and the
final prostration. Every step can be elaborate or as simple as a single flower and an honest
heart.

## What it is not

It is not idol-worship in the critique's sense, the image is a focus, not a cage. As Diana Eck's
classic study shows, the heart of the rite is *darśan*: the meeting of eyes between the devotee
and the form.
