*Dharma* comes from a root meaning "to hold", the holding-together of things. In the texts it
sweeps across scales: the dharma of the cosmos (order itself), of society (the king's justice), of
a stage of life, and svadharma, one's own particular duty, which the [Gītā](/wiki/scriptures/bhagavad-gita/)
famously tells Arjuna it is better to fail at than to succeed at another's.

## Why it resists translation

"Religion", "law", "duty" and "ethics" each capture a quarter of it. The epics treat dharma as
subtle, *sūkṣma*; the Mahābhārata's whole plot turns on good people disagreeing about what it
demands here, now, with these facts.

## On this site

Every practical page is quietly dharmic: eating for one's constitution, working without
grasping, honouring the teacher. The concept is the site's operating system; this entry is its
name.
