Classical India named four aims of life: wealth, pleasure, rightness, and mokṣa, release. The
first three have their uses and their satiations; the fourth answers a different hunger
altogether: the end of the wanderer's sense of being trapped in a role.

## What it is

The traditions differ instructively. For the Upaniṣadic tradition it is recognition, the self
was never bound, only mis-taken. For the devotional traditions it is unbroken relation, love
matured past separation. For the Buddhist traditions it is nirvāṇa, the fire's going-out for want
of fuel. What they share: mokṣa is available *now*, in this life, in principle.

## The path question

Which practice reaches it, this is why the traditions keep their many paths
([karma](/soul/action/), [gyan](/soul/knowledge/), [bhakti](/soul/devotion/)), and why the Gītā
ends by blessing all of them.
