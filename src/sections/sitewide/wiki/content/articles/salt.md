The kitchen keeps three salts, and they are not interchangeable in the cupboard even though
the family sometimes swaps rock and black for each other. Plain salt is the workhorse: it
seasons dal water, vegetables and rice through the cooking, added early so it melts into the
dish rather than sitting on it.

In the temple recipes hosted here, plain `salt` is what the day-to-day abhada cooking means;
the fasting kitchen never uses it. If a dish is for Ekadashi or Navratri, it takes [rock salt](/wiki/ingredients/rock-salt/) instead,
and the two must never be summed as one grocery.

The ayurvedic view: salty is one of the six tastes (lavana rasa), used in small measure to
kindle digestion and open taste; excess is blamed for aggravating pitta and kapha alike.
