South Indian masalas — sambar powder, saarina pudi, rasam powder — begin with whole
spices toasted in a dry pan until they darken a shade and the kitchen changes smell. The
roasting drives off moisture and wakes the volatile oils, so the ground blend keeps both
aroma and shelf life.

Each spice has its moment: coriander seeds the longest, chilies and lentils shorter,
[coconut](/wiki/ingredients/coconut/) (for blends that take it) until gold, sesame
and poppy seeds watched closely because they scorch. Cool completely before grinding, or
the powder cakes. The same logic, done in oil, is a different flavour — frying spices is
roux-adjacent, roasting them is clarification.
