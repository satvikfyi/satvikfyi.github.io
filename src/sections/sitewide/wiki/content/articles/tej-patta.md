What Indian recipes call bay leaf is **tej patta**, the leaf of the tamal tree
(*Cinnamomum tamala*) — a close cousin of cinnamon, with a warm, clove-like backbone. It is
not the Mediterranean bay laurel (*Laurus nobilis*) of Western kitchens, and the family's
standing correction is to never write plain "bay leaf" in a recipe: the canonical name on
this site is tej patta (Indian bay leaf).

Tej patta goes into hot ghee at the start of a tempering, alongside cumin and green
cardamom, for pulaos, dals and the Puri Rosaghara rice dishes; one or two leaves perfume
the fat, and patient frying until the leaf pales is what releases the aroma. In the
classical record the tamal tree's bark and leaf both appear — tamalaka among the aromatics
of early medical texts — long before "bay" entered any Indian kitchen vocabulary.
