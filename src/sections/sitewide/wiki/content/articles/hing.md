Hing is the dried latex tapped from the root of *Ferula* plants, usually sold ground and
cut with rice or wheat flour to tame it. Raw, it smells aggressive; cooked, a pinch in hot
ghee turns savoury and rounded — the backbone of the no-onion-no-garlic kitchen, which is
why Vaishnava and Jain cooking adopted it so completely.

Use it at the tempering stage: after the seeds crackle, a small pinch of hing in the hot fat
for a few seconds before the tempering meets the dal. Too much ruins a dish; a sixteenth of
a teaspoon is often plenty. In the ayurvedic frame it is sharply pungent and heating, the
classical friend of digestion and of vāta.
