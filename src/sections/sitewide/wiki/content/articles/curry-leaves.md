Curry leaves (*Murraya koenigii*) are not a garnish; they are a seasoning that happens
in the pan. They go into hot oil with [mustard seeds](/wiki/ingredients/mustard-seeds/) and
listen for the sizzle — the leaves' volatile aroma lifts into the fat and rides it into the
dal or vegetable dish.

The family rule: the leaf on the plate is eaten, not pushed aside. In the South the tree is
courtier to the kitchen door; the leaves also grind into chutneys and the pachadi of
festival meals, and a spray of them scents the buttermilk served at the end.
