Offer the food, and what returns is no longer just food; it is prasāda, "grace", the deity's
leftover transformed into blessing. The word also means serenity of mind; the double sense is
deliberate. In the classical sequence the deity receives (naivedya), the offering becomes
prasāda, and it is distributed to everyone present, often beginning with the youngest.

## Why it matters

The doctrine quietly reverses ordinary economy: one gives away in order to receive, and what one
receives is never "one's own" to begin with. Temple kitchens, the rice mountains of Tirupati,
the khichdi of Jagannātha Purī, run on it at civilization scale.

## At home

Cook as offering, eat as gift: it is the kitchen philosophy behind this site's
[satvik recipes](/body/meals/), and a good day's cooking becomes prasāda with a single sentence of
thanks (the [blog](/blog/) carries an essay on exactly this).
