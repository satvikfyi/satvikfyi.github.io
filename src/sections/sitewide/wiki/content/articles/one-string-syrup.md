Indian sweet-making is syrup-stage cooking, and the first stage is the one that
matters most: one-string, **ek taar**. Sugar (or [jaggery](/wiki/ingredients/jaggery/)) dissolves
with water and boils down until a drop pressed between thumb and finger pulls a single
thread when they part.

That thread is the binding stage — boondi meets it to become
laddu-bound, khaja's layers drink it, mysore pak sets against it. Cook
further and the syrup passes two-string toward crack stages, where brittler sweets live;
cook less and the sweet stays pourable. The test is the cook's fingers, and the family's
recipes assume them.
