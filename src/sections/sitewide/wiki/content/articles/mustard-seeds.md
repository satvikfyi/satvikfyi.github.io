Indian tempering defaults to dark-brown/black mustard seeds, with yellow a milder
cousin and rai's crackle the starting gun of
countless dishes. Whole seeds meet hot oil first, alone or with urad dal and
[curry leaves](/wiki/ingredients/curry-leaves/); when they stop popping, everything else follows.

Mustard is one of the five seeds of [panch phoron](/wiki/ingredients/panch-phoron/) and the pungent
heart of South Indian pickling. Its heat is oily and sudden rather than lingering — the
reason a tempering smells finished the moment the popping stops.
