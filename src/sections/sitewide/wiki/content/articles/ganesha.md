Gaṇeśa is the tradition's great democrat: the deity invoked before undertakings by Śaivas,
Vaiṣṇavas, Buddhists and Jains alike. Elephant-headed, round-bellied, riding a mouse, he carries
an axe (to cut attachment), a rope (to pull the seeker forward), and, in one broken tusk, the
story of his own scholarship, writing the Mahābhārata as Vyāsa dictated it.

## Why first

Mythologically the son of [Śiva](/wiki/gods/shiva/), honoured before all hosts (*gaṇa*),
Gaṇeśa receives the first offering of every [pūjā](/wiki/worship/puja/). Students whisper his
mantra before examinations; writers, before the first word. His festival, Gaṇeśa Caturthī, fills
Maharashtra's streets with clay images each rainy season.
