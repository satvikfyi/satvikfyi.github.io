Āratī is the moment the pūjā becomes visible: a camphor-or-wicked lamp circled clockwise before
the deity, three times or seven, while bells ring and the congregation sings. Then the flame is
carried to the worshippers, who cup their palms over it and draw the warmth to their eyes; light
taken from light.

## What it carries

The gesture is the whole theology in miniature: offering made, blessing returned, nothing lost.
Camphor burns without residue, the offering that leaves nothing behind but fragrance.

## In the home

The evening āratī, often the hymn *Om Jai Jagdish Hare*, is many Indian childhoods' fixed point:
the lamp circling in a parent's hand, the smell of camphor, everyone's eyes briefly bright.
