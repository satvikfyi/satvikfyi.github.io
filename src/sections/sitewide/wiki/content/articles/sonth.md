Sonth is ginger rhizome dried and ground — called **saunth** in Hindi, **sukku** or
chukku in the South. Drying concentrates and heats it: where [fresh ginger](/wiki/ingredients/ginger/) cooks
into a dish, sonth seasons it, and the two are separate groceries that never substitute
one-for-one.

It stars in the fasting and festival kitchen — saunth ki chutney with jaggery and tamarind,
panakam's dry-ginger bite, the panchamrit of South Indian temples — and in spice blends
where its powder disperses evenly. Kanchipuram's famous temple idli is seasoned with it,
a reminder that the old sanctum kitchens took their heat from ginger and pepper rather
than chili.
