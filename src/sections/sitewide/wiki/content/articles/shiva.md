Śiva is the paradox held still: the great ascetic wrapped in silence on Kailāsa, and Natarāja, the
king of dancers whose ring of fire is the universe's rhythm. He is worshipped as the lingam, the
undifferentiated mark of the formless, and loved in a thousand folk forms.

## What he carries

The crescent moon (time), the Ganges (grace descending), the drum and the fire (creation and
destruction), the third eye (seeing through appearances). His vehicle is the bull Nandin; his
consort, in her own right a great deity, is [Devī](/wiki/gods/devi/).

## In the living tradition

Śiva is Ādinātha, the first teacher of yoga; the [Yoga Sūtras](/wiki/scriptures/yoga-sutras/)
name him among yoga's exemplars, and Śivarātri's night of chanting keeps his mantra; oṁ namaḥ
śivāya, among the most sung on earth (the site's [mantra collection](/mind/mantras/) carries it).
