Tadka — baghar, chhaunk, thalippu — is the subcontinent's signature move: whole spices
met with hot fat so their aromatic oils release, then the sizzling fat and everything in it
poured over the dal, sambar or vegetable dish at the end, or the dish started in it.

The order is a grammar. Hard seeds first ([mustard](/wiki/ingredients/mustard-seeds/), cumin) until
they crackle; then dals and dried red chili for colour; then leaves
([curry leaves](/wiki/ingredients/curry-leaves/), [tej patta](/wiki/ingredients/tej-patta/)); and
[hing](/wiki/ingredients/hing/) last and briefly, because it burns fastest of all. Ghee is the
satvik medium, mustard oil the eastern one, and the pan should be ready for the pouring the
moment the last ingredient flowers — a tadka that sits loses its voice.

It is also the technique that makes no-onion-no-garlic cooking taste complete: the bloomed
spices supply the savoury depth alliums otherwise give.
