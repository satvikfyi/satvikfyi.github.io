Jaggery is cane (or palm) juice boiled and set without refining, keeping its molasses,
minerals and a butterscotch depth white sugar lacks. In the South it appears as **kandasari**
or naatu sakkarai; Bengal prizes **nolen gur**, the date-palm version, for its winter
sandalwood notes.

Temple kitchens reach for jaggery where sweets carry meaning — [payasam](/wiki/cooking-techniques/milk-reduction/), sakkarai pongal,
the jaggery-water drinks of festival days. It melts unevenly, so recipes strain the syrup
after dissolving, and it behaves differently from sugar at the [syrup stages](/wiki/cooking-techniques/one-string-syrup/), setting softer.
