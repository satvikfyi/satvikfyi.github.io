Rock salt goes by many names on packets and in kitchens — **sendha namak**, **saindhava
lavana**, pink rock salt — and all of them mean the same mined, unrefined crystal. It is the
only salt of the phalahari (fasting) kitchen: on Ekadashi and Navratri days the food is cooked
with rock salt, never plain salt.

The family's rule of thumb: rock salt and [black salt](/wiki/ingredients/black-salt/) are
sometimes used interchangeably, because both are chosen to impart *flavour*, not only
saltiness. Rock salt reads gentler and faintly mineral; it finishes chaats, raitas and
buttermilk as much as it cooks sabudana.

Saindhava also carries weight in the ayurvedic tradition as the salt of choice — considered
lightest of the salts and agreeable to the eyes, which is why classical texts single it out
among lavana varieties.
