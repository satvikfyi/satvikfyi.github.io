The cashew is Brazilian, carried east by the Portuguese, and nowhere did it travel
further into a cuisine than India — ground into the white gravies of the North, whole into
pulao and [kheer](/wiki/cooking-techniques/milk-reduction/), and famously inside the Tirupati laddu's
modern dittam.

That adoption is exactly why it marks a line in temple food: the archaic Rosaghara canon at
Puri still bars it from sanctum offerings along with the [potato](/wiki/ingredients/potato/),
while 19th- and 20th-century dittams elsewhere welcomed it. Both facts are true, and recipes
here note which side of the line they stand on.
