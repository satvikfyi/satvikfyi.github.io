Karma means, simply, action, and by extension the trace an action leaves, seeding future
experience. The classical doctrine has three parts: every intentional act has consequences;
those consequences shape character, circumstance and (in the traditions that accept rebirth) the
next birth, and the process is a law, not a judge.

## What it is not

It is not fate, the past constrains, the present is free. Nor is it divine punishment: no one
administers karma, just as no one administers gravity. And the Gītā's great insight, taught on
this site's [karma yoga](/soul/action/) pages, is that action done without attachment leaves no
binding trace.

## Why it matters here

Karma makes the tradition's practices coherent: [meditation](/mind/meditation/) and
[service](/soul/action/) are not bribes to heaven but changes to the doer.
