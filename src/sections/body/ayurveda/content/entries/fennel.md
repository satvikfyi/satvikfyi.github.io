indications:
  - The classic carminative, bloating, gas, post-meal heaviness
  - Gentle enough that tradition gives it (carefully, diluted) even to colicky infants; ask a practitioner first
  - Traditionally regarded as clearant and cooling for the eyes
  - Mildly supportive of milk flow in nursing mothers (traditional use)
contraindications:
  - Medicinal doses in pregnancy, culinary chewing is traditional and fine
  - Allergy to celery-family plants (rare)
preparation: "Chew ½ tsp of seeds after a meal, the saunf-and-mishri bowl exists for this. As tea:
  steep 1 tsp of lightly crushed seeds in hot water for 5 minutes. Fennel also mellows pickles and
  complements fish dishes in coastal tradition."

Few herbs make medicine as pleasant as miśreya: the sweet anise brightness is the after-dinner
"mouth freshener" of a billion meals, doing quiet carminative work while it refreshes. Vāta's
bubbles and pitta's burn both settle before the plate is cleared.
