indications:
  - Aids digestion at the end of a meal, the classical "last course"
  - Traditional support for grahaṇī (irregular, IBS-like digestion)
  - Lightens what heavy food leaves behind; refreshes without cold
  - The meal-drink of choice in hot, kapha-aggravating weather
contraindications:
  - Very dry, depleted or injured states; the texts limit takra there
  - Deep-freezing climates and mid-winter excess, per the classical cautions
  - Lactose sensitivity, take the traditional spices with warm water instead
preparation: Churn one part fresh yogurt with three parts water until thin and slightly frothy; stir
  in roasted cumin, a pinch of rock salt, grated ginger and curry leaves. Sip at room temperature
  after lunch, never iced. (See the takra recipe in the meals collection.)

Takra transforms yogurt, heavy, channel-clogging in excess, the tradition warns; into its own
opposite: light, scraping and refreshing. The churning is the medicine. One glass, spiced, after the
midday meal is one of the oldest digestives still in daily use.
