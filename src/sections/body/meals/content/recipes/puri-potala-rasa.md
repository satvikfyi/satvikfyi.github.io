ingredients:
  - item: "pointed gourds (parwal)"
    quantity: 8
    note: "lightly scraped, slit lengthwise at both ends; parwal/potala"
  - item: "coconut milk"
    quantity: "1 cup"
    note: "fresh thick first-press"
  - item: "cumin seeds"
    quantity: "1 tbsp"
  - item: "ginger"
    quantity: "1 inch piece"
    note: "peeled"
  - item: "turmeric powder"
    quantity: "1/2 tsp"
  - item: "red chili powder"
    quantity: "1/2 tsp"
  - item: "ghee"
    quantity: "2 tbsp"
  - item: "tej patta (Indian bay leaf)"
    quantity: 1
  - item: "green cardamom"
    quantity: 2
    note: "lightly crushed"
  - item: "water"
    quantity: "1/2 cup"
  - item: "salt"
    quantity: "1 tsp"

steps:
  - "Grind the cumin seeds and fresh ginger together with 2 tablespoons of water into a fine paste."
  - "Heat 1 tablespoon of ghee in a pan over medium heat; add the prepared pointed gourds and fry for 5 minutes until lightly golden on all sides, then remove."
  - "Add the remaining 1 tablespoon of ghee to the pan."
  - "Add the crushed cardamoms and tej patta; sauté for 30 seconds until fragrant."
  - "Add the ground cumin-ginger paste; cook on medium-low flame for 2 minutes until raw aroma dissipates."
  - "Stir in the turmeric powder, red chili powder, salt, and 1/2 cup of water; simmer for 3 minutes."
  - "Add the fried pointed gourds and pour in the thick coconut milk."
  - "Reduce heat to low, cover tightly, and simmer gently for 8 minutes until the gourds are tender and the gravy is rich."
  - "Remove from the heat and rest for 5 minutes before serving with steamed rice."
---

Potala Rasa demonstrates the coastal Odia temple technique of building a luxurious sauce without alliums or cashew pastes. Fresh thick coconut milk provides emulsion viscosity, while pointed gourd (potala) absorbs the delicate aromatics of crushed green cardamom and ginger.
