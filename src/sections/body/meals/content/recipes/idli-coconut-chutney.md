  - Grease the idli plates lightly and fill each mould three-quarters full with the fermented batter.
  - Steam for 10–12 minutes until a skewer comes out clean; rest 2 minutes before unmoulding.
  - For the chutney, grind the coconut, roasted gram, chili, ginger, tamarind and salt with a little water into a coarse paste.
  - Temper mustard seeds and curry leaves in a teaspoon of ghee and pour over the chutney.
  - Serve the idlis warm with ghee melting over them and the chutney alongside.

Idli is one of the world's oldest steamed and fermented foods, and temple kitchens in the south have
served it for centuries as quintessential satvik fare, no onion, no garlic, nothing stale.
Fermentation partly pre-digests the rice and urad dal, which is why ayurveda considers a well-made
idli lighter than a fried or flour-based breakfast, and coconut keeps the plate cooling for pitta.
