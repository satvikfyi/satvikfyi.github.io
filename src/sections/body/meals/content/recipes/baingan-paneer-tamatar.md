ingredients:
  - item: "eggplant"
    quantity: "1 large"
    note: "sliced into 1/2-inch rounds, shallow-fried golden in ghee"
  - item: "paneer"
    quantity: "200 g"
    note: "sliced into flat 1/4-inch slabs"
  - item: "tomato"
    quantity: 3
    note: "large ripe tomatoes, sliced into 1/4-inch rounds"
  - item: "ghee"
    quantity: "2 tbsp"
  - item: "oregano (dried)"
    quantity: "1 tsp"
    note: "or dried basil"
  - item: "black peppercorns"
    quantity: "1/2 tsp"
    note: "freshly cracked"
  - item: "salt"
    quantity: "1 tsp"

steps:
  - "Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F) and lightly grease a baking dish with 1 teaspoon of ghee."
  - "Arrange a single layer of the golden pan-fried eggplant rounds in the bottom of the dish."
  - "Place a layer of sliced paneer slabs over the eggplant."
  - "Top with a layer of sliced fresh tomato rounds."
  - "Season the layers evenly with salt, freshly cracked black pepper, and dried oregano."
  - "Repeat layers as space permits, finishing with a layer of tomato rounds and herbs on top."
  - "Dot the surface with the remaining ghee."
  - "Bake uncovered at 190°C (375°F) for 25 minutes until the tomatoes soften, release juices, and brown lightly around the edges."
  - "Remove from the oven, rest for 5 minutes, slice into squares, and serve warm."
---

Introduced to Vaishnava temple cooking by Kurma Dasa during international preaching tours in Australia and Europe, this baked dish bridges Western casserole mechanics with strict Satvik dietary laws. Caramelized eggplant starches, lactic milk curds, and sweet baked tomato juices fuse into a savory layered course without onions or garlic.
