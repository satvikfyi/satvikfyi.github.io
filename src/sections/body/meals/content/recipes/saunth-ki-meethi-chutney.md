steps:
  - "In a heavy saucepan, combine the tamarind extract, grated jaggery, and 1 cup of water."
  - "Bring to a boil over medium heat, stirring continuously until the jaggery dissolves completely."
  - "Pass the hot syrup through a fine mesh sieve into a clean saucepan to eliminate any fibrous cane or tamarind residues."
  - "Return the strained syrup to medium-low flame."
  - "Whisk in the dry ginger powder (saunth), roasted cumin powder, black salt, red chili powder, and hing."
  - "Simmer gently for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the chutney thickens and coats the back of a metal spoon."
  - "Remove from the flame and let cool completely (the sauce will thicken further as it cools)."
  - "Transfer to a sterile glass jar; store at room temperature for up to a month or refrigerated for several months."

Saunth Ki Meethi Chutney is the hallmark condiment of North Indian festive cooking, particularly across the temples of Vrindavan and Mathura. It utilizes sun-dried ginger root powder (saunth)—which carries warming, pungent properties without the moisture of fresh ginger—blended with local cane jaggery and sour tamarind to aid digestion when eating rich, deep-fried offerings.
