steps:
  - "Heat the wood-pressed sesame oil in a heavy, deep saucepan over medium heat."
  - "Add the mustard seeds and allow them to crackle."
  - "Add the chana dal, urad dal, raw peanuts, dried red chilies, and curry leaves; sauté on medium-low flame until the lentils and peanuts turn deep golden-brown."
  - "Add the hing and stir for 10 seconds."
  - "Pour in the thick tamarind extract, turmeric powder, and salt (take caution: the hot oil will splatter)."
  - "Bring to a boil, then lower the heat to medium-low."
  - "Simmer uncovered for 15 to 18 minutes, stirring frequently, until the tamarind water evaporates, the paste thickens into a glossy concentrate, and oil visibly separates from the edges."
  - "Stir in the freshly roasted fenugreek-sesame spice powder and cook for an additional 2 minutes."
  - "Remove from the flame and cool completely before spooning into sterile jars or folding into freshly cooked rice."

Pulikachal is the concentrated mother-sauce of the South Indian temple kitchen. Developed centuries ago by Sri Vaishnava cooks to sustain pilgrims traveling on foot between Divya Desams, this reduction of aged tamarind and cold-pressed sesame oil serves as the flavoring foundation for Kovil Puliyodharai.
