ingredients:

    quantity: 1 cup
    note: coarse
  - item: ghee
    quantity: 2 tbsp
  - item: mustard seeds
    quantity: ½ tsp
  - item: cumin seeds
    quantity: ½ tsp
  - item: curry leaves
    quantity: 8
    note: fresh
  - item: 'ginger'
    quantity: 1 tsp
    note: grated
  - item: green chili
    quantity: 1
    note: optional, slit
  - item: carrot
    quantity: 1
    note: diced small
  - item: 'green peas'
    quantity: ¼ cup
  - item: water
    quantity: 2½ cups
  - item: rock salt
    quantity: to taste
  - item: lemon
    quantity: 1 tsp
    note: juice
  - item: coconut (fresh grated)
    quantity: 2 tbsp
    note: to garnish
steps:
  - Dry-roast the rava in a pan over medium heat until it is warm to the touch and smells lightly toasted; set aside.
  - Heat the ghee, pop the mustard seeds, then add cumin, curry leaves, ginger and the optional chili.
  - Add the diced carrot and peas with a splash of water; cook 3–4 minutes until just tender.
  - Pour in the water with salt and bring to a rolling boil, then lower the heat.
  - Rain the roasted rava in slowly with one hand while stirring constantly with the other, so no lumps form.
  - Cover and cook on the lowest heat 3–4 minutes; fluff, then finish with lemon juice and coconut.

Upma belongs to the everyday South Indian satvik kitchen, temple kitchens across Tamil Nadu and
Karnataka serve versions of it precisely because it is mild, fresh and quick. The constant stirring
at the end is not just technique: it "cooks in" the water, leaving the semolina light rather than
paste-like, which keeps the dish easy on the digestion.
