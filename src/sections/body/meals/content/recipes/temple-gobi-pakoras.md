ingredients:
  - item: "cauliflower"
    quantity: "3 cups"
    note: "cut into bite-sized florets, parboiled 2 minutes, patted completely dry"
  - item: "besan (gram flour)"
    note: "chickpea flour"
    quantity: "1 cup"
  - item: "rice flour"
    quantity: "2 tbsp"
    note: "adds extra crunch"
  - item: "turmeric powder"
    quantity: "1/2 tsp"
  - item: "cumin seeds"
    quantity: "1 tsp"
  - item: "hing (asafoetida)"
    quantity: "1/4 tsp"
  - item: "water"
    quantity: "3/4 cup"
    note: "for batter"
  - item: "oil"
    quantity: "3 cups"
    note: "or ghee, for deep-frying"
  - item: "salt"
    quantity: "1 tsp"

steps:
  - "In a wide bowl, whisk together the besan, rice flour, turmeric powder, cumin seeds, hing, salt, and 3/4 cup of water into a smooth batter that coats the back of a spoon."
  - "Let the batter rest for 10 minutes to allow the chickpea flour to hydrate."
  - "Heat 3 cups of oil or ghee in a deep karai over medium-high heat until hot."
  - "Dip the dry parboiled cauliflower florets into the batter, ensuring each floret is evenly coated."
  - "Carefully drop the batter-coated florets one by one into the hot oil without overcrowding the pan."
  - "Fry over medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes, turning occasionally, until the coating turns crisp, puffed, and golden-brown."
  - "Remove with a slotted skimmer and drain on paper towels."
  - "Serve hot accompanied by fresh mint-coriander chutney or sweet date chutney."
---

Pakoras are an essential component of the international Hare Krishna Sunday Feast. Adding a small measure of fine rice flour to the besan batter creates an outer barrier that resists oil absorption, ensuring the cauliflower interior steams to tenderness while the exterior remains crisp.
