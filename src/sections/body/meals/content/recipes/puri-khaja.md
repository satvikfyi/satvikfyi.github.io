steps:
  - "In a wide bowl, rub 4 tablespoons of melted ghee into the maida with your fingertips until crumbly."
  - "Add water gradually and knead into a stiff, smooth dough; cover and rest for 20 minutes."
  - "Cream together 2 tablespoons of ghee and 2 tablespoons of rice flour into a smooth, spreadable paste (satta)."
  - "Roll out the rested dough on a lightly floured board into a very thin, large rectangular sheet."
  - "Smear a thin, uniform layer of the satta paste across the entire surface of the rolled dough sheet."
  - "Roll the dough tightly from one edge into a compact, Swiss-roll-style cylindrical log."
  - "Slice the rolled log into 1-inch thick diagonal pinwheels."
  - "Gently flatten each slice with a rolling pin into an elongated oval ribbon (about 4 inches long), keeping layers intact."
  - "In a saucepan, boil the sugar, 1 cup of water, and cardamom powder until it reaches a 1.5-string sticky syrup consistency; keep warm."
  - "Heat 3 cups of ghee in a deep karai over low heat."
  - "Fry the layered ribbons in batches over gentle, steady low heat for 8 to 10 minutes, allowing the layers to blossom into crisp, pale-golden sheets."
  - "Drain the fried khajas and immediately submerge in the warm sugar syrup for 2 minutes."
  - "Transfer to a wire rack to drain; the sugar syrup will set into a crisp glaze as the pastry cools."
---

Khaja is the foremost dry prasadam (Sukhila Mahaprasad) of Jagannath Puri. Pilgrims transport boxes of these layered pastries thousands of miles across India without refrigeration. Deep-frying over slow heat in pure cow's ghee separates the laminated flour sheets, creating an airy, brittle crunch.
