steps:
  - "Place the freshly grated coconut, roasted gram (dalia), green chilies, sliced ginger, and salt into a blender jar."
  - "Add 1/2 cup of water and blend to a semi-smooth, thick paste."
  - "Transfer the ground chutney into a ceramic or stainless steel serving bowl."
  - "Heat the sesame oil in a small tempering ladle over medium heat."
  - "Add the mustard seeds and urad dal; fry until the mustard crackles and the dal turns golden-brown."
  - "Add the fresh curry leaves and hing; sizzle for 10 seconds until aromatic."
  - "Pour the hot sizzling oil tempering directly over the blended chutney."
  - "Gently fold the tempering into the top layer of the chutney before serving alongside hot idlis, dosas, or pongal."

At the massive Annadanam halls of Tirumala, fresh coconut chutney is prepared daily in monumental stone grinders. Without onions, garlic, or excessive chilies, the richness of fresh coconut meat is grounded by roasted Bengal gram (pottu kadalai), while wood-pressed gingelly oil and popping mustard seeds provide the signature temple finish.
