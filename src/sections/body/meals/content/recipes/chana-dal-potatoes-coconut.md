  - item: "chana dal"

  - item: "potato"

    note: "peeled and cut into 1/2-inch cubes"
  - item: "coconut (fresh grated)"
    quantity: "2 tbsp"
    note: "thinly sliced into small flakes"
  - item: "turmeric powder"
    quantity: "1/2 tsp"
  - item: "ghee"
    quantity: "2 tbsp"
  - item: "cumin seeds"

  - item: "dry red chili"
    quantity: 1
    note: "halved"

    quantity: 1

    quantity: "1.5 tsp"
  - item: "water"
    quantity: "4 cups"

steps:
  - "In a heavy pot, combine the soaked chana dal, water, turmeric powder, and 1 teaspoon of salt."
  - "Simmer over medium heat for 30 minutes until the dal is tender to the touch but still holds its distinct shape."
  - "In a separate saucepan, boil the diced potatoes in salted water for 8 minutes until tender; drain."
  - "Heat the ghee in a small frying pan over medium heat; add the fresh coconut flakes and fry until deep golden-brown, then remove with a slotted spoon."
  - "In the remaining hot ghee, add the cumin seeds, tej patta, and dried red chili; fry until fragrant."
  - "Add the hing and sauté for 10 seconds."
  - "Pour the hot ghee and spices into the pot of cooked chana dal."
  - "Stir in the drained potato cubes, garam masala, amchur powder, and remaining salt."
  - "Simmer over low heat for 5 minutes to allow the flavors to penetrate the potatoes."
  - "Garnish with the reserved golden coconut flakes and serve warm."
---

In Vaishnava feast service, Chana Dal holds its shape better than mung or toor lentils, providing substantial texture. Combining sweet, earthy Bengal gram with sour mango powder (amchur) and the nuttiness of ghee-browned fresh coconut flakes mimics the complex flavor profile of festive temple banquets.
