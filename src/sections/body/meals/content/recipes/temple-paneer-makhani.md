steps:
  - "Heat the ghee and 1 tablespoon of butter in a saucepan over medium flame."
  - "Add the ginger paste and hing; sauté for 30 seconds until fragrant."
  - "Add the strained tomato puree, Kashmiri red chili powder, sugar, and salt."
  - "Simmer over medium-low heat for 12 to 14 minutes, stirring frequently, until the puree concentrates into a thick base and fat separates."
  - "Whisk in the heavy cream and crushed kasuri methi until smooth and velvety."
  - "Slide the paneer cubes into the simmering sauce."
  - "Cook over low heat for 3 minutes to allow the cheese to soften and absorb the butter-rich sauce."
  - "Stir in the remaining 1 tablespoon of butter off the flame until melted."
  - "Rest covered for 5 minutes before serving with steamed basmati rice or warm parathas."

While commercial restaurant makhani sauces depend heavily on garlic and onion foundations, the temple adaptation relies on slow-reduced strained tomatoes, generous fresh butter, and sun-dried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi). The lactones in fenugreek provide an unmistakable savory depth that binds the sweet tomato acidity.
