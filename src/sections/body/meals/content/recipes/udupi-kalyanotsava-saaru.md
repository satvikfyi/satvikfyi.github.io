steps:
  - "Coarsely crush black peppercorns and cumin seeds together in a mortar."
  - "In a deep vessel, combine tamarind water, grated jaggery, turmeric powder, salt, and half the crushed pepper-cumin mixture."
  - "Simmer over medium flame for 7 minutes until raw tamarind aroma disappears."
  - "Pour in decanted clear toor dal broth and bring to a gentle simmer."
  - "Watch closely and remove from heat the instant a single rolling froth forms across surface."
  - "Heat ghee in a small ladle over medium flame; add mustard seeds and let them splutter."
  - "Add remaining crushed pepper-cumin, curry leaves, and hing; fry for 15 seconds until aromatic."
  - "Pour sizzling ghee tempering directly into warm saaru."
  - "Cover immediately with a tight lid to trap volatile aromatics, and rest for 5 minutes before serving."
---

Kalyanotsava Saaru is reserved for grand temple marriage ceremonies and festival banquets in the Madhwa tradition. By utilizing only the decanted starch-rich liquor (kattu) of slow-boiled toor dal rather than the mashed pulse, the broth remains clear while carrying deep peppercorn and cumin heat.
