steps:
  - "In a thick-bottomed pan, melt the grated jaggery with 3 tablespoons of water over medium heat."
  - "Strain the melted jaggery syrup through a sieve to eliminate any impurities, then return to the pan."
  - "Add the cooked, drained whole chana dal and the grated dry coconut (copra) to the simmering syrup."
  - "Cook over medium-low heat, stirring frequently with a wooden spoon for 12 to 15 minutes, until the dal absorbs the jaggery and the mixture thickens."
  - "Heat the ghee in a small skillet; fry the cashews until golden-brown."
  - "Pour the hot ghee and cashews directly into the thickened dal halva."
  - "Stir continuously over low flame until the halva turns lustrous, glossy, and begins to pull cleanly away from the edges of the pan."
  - "Stir in the freshly ground cardamom powder and the speck of edible camphor."
  - "Remove from the heat and serve warm."

Legend connects this sacred sweet directly to Sri Vadiraja Tirtha of the Sodhe Matha in Udupi during the 16th century. Vadiraja would hold a tray of this sweet Bengal gram preparation above his head, and Lord Hayagriva (the horse-headed incarnation of Vishnu) would appear to partake of the offering, leaving a portion behind as sanctified prasadam.
