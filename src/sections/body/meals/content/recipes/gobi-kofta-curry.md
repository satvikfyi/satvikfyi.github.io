ingredients:
  - item: "cauliflower"
    quantity: "2 cups"
    note: "finely grated, squeezed bone-dry in a cloth"
  - item: "besan (gram flour)"
    note: "chickpea flour"
    quantity: "1/2 cup"
  - item: "ginger"
    quantity: "1 tbsp"
    note: "divided: 1 tsp for kofta, remainder for gravy"
  - item: "red chili powder"
    quantity: "1/2 tsp"
  - item: "oil"
    quantity: "2 cups"
    note: "for deep-frying"
  - item: "tomato"
    quantity: 3
    note: "pureed smooth"
  - item: "ghee"
    quantity: "2 tbsp"
  - item: "cumin seeds"
    quantity: "1 tsp"
  - item: "hing (asafoetida)"
    quantity: "1/4 tsp"
  - item: "coriander powder"
    quantity: "1 tsp"
  - item: "garam masala"
    quantity: "1/2 tsp"
  - item: "water"
    quantity: "1 cup"
  - item: "salt"
    quantity: "1.5 tsp"

steps:
  - "In a bowl, combine the dry grated cauliflower, besan, 1 teaspoon grated ginger, red chili powder, and 1/2 teaspoon salt into a stiff dough."
  - "Roll the cauliflower dough into small 1-inch spherical balls."
  - "Heat the oil in a deep karai over medium-high heat; deep-fry the kofta balls in batches for 4 to 5 minutes until deep golden-brown; drain."
  - "Heat the ghee in a separate saucepan over medium heat; add cumin seeds, remaining ginger, and hing until crackling."
  - "Add the pureed tomatoes, coriander powder, and remaining 1 teaspoon salt; cook for 8 minutes until the gravy thickens and oil beads form."
  - "Pour in 1 cup of water and simmer the sauce over medium-low flame for 5 minutes."
  - "Stir in the garam masala and remove the gravy from the heat."
  - "Gently lower the warm fried koftas into the sauce immediately before serving so they remain crisp outside and soft inside."
---

Gobi Kofta is a signature dish of ISKCON Sunday feast banquets. Cauliflower's natural sweetness concentrates when grated and fried within a chickpea batter, offering a savory dumpling that absorbs the tart tomato-hing gravy upon plating.
