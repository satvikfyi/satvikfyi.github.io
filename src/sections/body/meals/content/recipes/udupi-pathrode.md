steps:
  - "Drain the soaked rice and toor dal; place in a blender jar with dried red chilies, coriander seeds, cumin seeds, tamarind paste, jaggery, hing, and salt."
  - "Grind with minimal water into a thick, coarse, spreadable masala paste."
  - "Place a large colocasia leaf flat on a clean board, vein-side up."
  - "Smear a thin, even layer of the masala paste over the entire leaf surface."
  - "Place a second leaf on top and smear with paste; repeat with 4 to 5 leaves."
  - "Fold the leaf edges inward and roll tightly from the base into a compact cylindrical log."
  - "Place the rolled leaf logs into a steamer basket over boiling water; steam for 30 minutes until the leaves and lentil paste are fully cooked through."
  - "Remove from the steamer and let cool completely, then slice into 1/2-inch round pinwheels."
  - "Heat the coconut oil in a cast-iron skillet over medium flame; pan-sear the pinwheels for 3 minutes per side until crisp and golden-brown."
  - "Serve warm as a crisp tea-time or festival accompaniment."

Pathrode (or Patra) is an ancient monsoon delicacy of the Konkan and coastal Karnataka Vaishnava mathas. Taro leaves (kesuvina ele) are layered with a spiced lentil paste balancing sweet jaggery, sour tamarind, and fiery chilies. The tamarind's tartness neutralizes natural oxalate crystals, yielding a tender, savory roll.
