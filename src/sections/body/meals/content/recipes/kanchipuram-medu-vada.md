ingredients:
  - item: "urad dal"
    quantity: "1 cup"
    note: "whole skinless black gram, soaked 2 hours, drained"
  - item: "black peppercorns"
    quantity: "1 tbsp"
    note: "coarsely crushed"
  - item: "cumin seeds"
    quantity: "1 tsp"
  - item: "ginger"
    quantity: "1 inch piece"
    note: "finely minced"
  - item: "hing (asafoetida)"
    quantity: "1/4 tsp"
  - item: "curry leaves"
    quantity: "1 sprig"
    note: "finely torn"
  - item: "oil"
    quantity: "3 cups"
    note: "for deep-frying"
  - item: "salt"
    quantity: "1 tsp"

steps:
  - "Grind the drained urad dal in a wet grinder or blender with 2 to 3 tablespoons of ice-cold water into a thick, fluffy batter."
  - "Transfer the batter to a wide bowl; beat vigorously in one direction with your hand for 3 minutes to incorporate air until a drop of batter floats in water."
  - "Fold in the crushed black peppercorns, cumin seeds, minced ginger, torn curry leaves, hing, and salt."
  - "Heat 3 cups of oil in a deep karai over medium flame until it registers 180°C (350°F)."
  - "Wet your palms with water, take a lemon-sized ball of batter, shape into a smooth round, and press a hole through the center with your thumb."
  - "Gently slide the shaped vada into the hot oil."
  - "Fry 3 to 4 vadas at a time over medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes, turning occasionally, until uniformly crisp and deep golden-brown."
  - "Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels."
  - "Serve immediately while crisp and hot."
---

At the Sri Varadaraja Perumal Temple in Kanchipuram, Medu Vada is offered during early morning thaligai. Temple vadas strictly omit onions, relying entirely on bruised peppercorns, fresh ginger, and hing to provide sharp warmth and digestive balance against the dense protein of black gram.
