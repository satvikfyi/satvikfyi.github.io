steps:
  - "Whisk the besan and 1.25 cups of water together in a bowl into a smooth, lump-free pouring batter of medium consistency."
  - "In a heavy pot, combine the sugar and 1 cup of water; boil over medium flame until it reaches a soft single-string syrup consistency (108°C / 226°F); keep warm."
  - "Heat 3 cups of cow's ghee in a heavy karai over medium-high heat."
  - "Hold a perforated boondi ladle above the hot ghee; pour a ladle of batter over the perforations, letting round droplets fall into the fat."
  - "Fry the boondi for only 45 to 60 seconds until cooked through but soft and pale yellow (not crunchy)."
  - "Remove immediately with a skimmer and drop the warm boondi directly into the warm sugar syrup."
  - "Repeat until all batter is fried, stirring the boondi into the syrup so every droplet absorbs the sweet liquor."
  - "Heat 1 tablespoon of ghee in a small pan; fry the cashews and golden raisins until golden and plump."
  - "Stir the fried cashews, raisins, cardamom powder, crushed edible camphor, and kalkandu crystals into the boondi mixture."
  - "Pulse one-third of the soaked boondi lightly in a food processor to release binding starches, then return it to the bowl."
  - "While the mixture is still warm, grease your palms with ghee and compress handfuls into firm, compact round laddus."
  - "Allow the laddus to cool and set on a tray for 1 hour before serving."

The Tirupati Laddu possesses a Geographical Indication (GI) status under Indian law. Introduced formally into the temple offerings in August 1715, its preparation is strictly governed by the temple Dittam (sanctified ingredient balance), which mandates precise measures of gram flour, cow's ghee, sugar syrup, crystal candies, and genuine edible camphor.
