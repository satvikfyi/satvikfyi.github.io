steps:
  - "Combine the soaked green moong beans, water, turmeric powder, and 1 teaspoon of salt in a pressure cooker."
  - "Cook for 4 whistles (about 15 minutes) until the beans are soft, tender, and creamy when pressed."
  - "Heat the butter or ghee in a saucepan over medium heat."
  - "Add the cumin seeds and allow them to sizzle and darken slightly."
  - "Add the grated ginger, slit green chilies, and hing; sauté for 30 seconds."
  - "Pour in the pureed tomatoes and remaining 1/2 teaspoon of salt."
  - "Cook the tomato mixture over medium heat for 6 to 8 minutes until it reduces to a fragrant, thick paste."
  - "Pour the cooked whole green moong beans along with their broth into the tomato masala."
  - "Simmer over low flame for 10 minutes to allow the beans to absorb the seasoned base."
  - "Stir in the garam masala and fresh cilantro, cover for 5 minutes, and serve hot."

Whole green mung (sabut moong) is revered across Ayurvedic traditions as the king of pulses, recognized in the Charaka Samhita for its restorative, non-gas-forming nature. In temple dining, finishing the dal with a dollop of fresh unsalted cow's butter (makhan) yields a rich mouthfeel without heavy cream.
