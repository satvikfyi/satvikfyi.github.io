steps:
  - Soak the tamarind in a cup of hot water for 10 minutes and squeeze out the juice; discard the solids.
  - Simmer the tamarind water with the tomato, turmeric, jaggery and salt for 8–10 minutes until the raw smell mellows.
  - Stir in the freshly ground pepper and roasted cumin.
  - Heat the ghee, pop the mustard seeds, then add cumin, asafoetida and curry leaves; pour into the pot.
  - Add the remaining hot water, bring just to a frothy simmer (do not boil hard), and finish with coriander. Serve as a soup or poured over rice.

Rasam is what South Indian homes pour when someone is coming down with something: pepper and cumin
to kindle agni, tamarind to gentle it, all in a broth thin enough for the weakest appetite. The
satvik version simply leaves out the garlic most restaurant rasams carry, and loses nothing of
the warmth. The frothy, never-boiled finish keeps the pepper's edge bright.
