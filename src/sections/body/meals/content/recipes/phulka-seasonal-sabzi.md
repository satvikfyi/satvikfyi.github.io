    quantity: 1 cup

  - item: water
    quantity: ⅓ cup

  - item: ghee
    quantity: 1 tsp

  - item: ghee
    quantity: 1 tbsp
  - item: cumin seeds

  - item: turmeric powder

  - item: asafoetida (hing)

  - item: mixed vegetables
    quantity: 2 cups
    note: seasonal, e.g. carrot, beans, peas, or lauki, potato, cabbage
  - item: coriander powder
    quantity: 1 tsp
  - item: rock salt
    quantity: to taste
  - item: coriander leaves
    quantity: 1 tbsp
    note: chopped
steps:
  - Knead the flour with water into a soft, pliable dough; rest it 15 minutes under a damp cloth.
  - Divide into 6 balls; roll each into a thin disc on a floured board.
  - Cook each disc on a hot tava until spots appear, then puff it directly over the flame for a few seconds per side; brush with the optional ghee and stack under a cloth.
  - For the sabzi, heat the ghee, pop the cumin, and add asafoetida and turmeric.
  - Add the vegetables with 2 tablespoons of water, coriander powder and salt; cover and cook until just tender, 6–10 minutes depending on the vegetable.
  - Finish with coriander leaves and serve the phulkas warm with the sabzi.

There is no simpler satvik meal than this: freshly rolled wheat bread, dry-puffed on the flame, and
whatever the season offers from the vegetable basket. Charaka's dietary advice keeps returning to
this idea, eat what is local, seasonal and customary for you, freshly made. The phulka's char
spots and the sabzi's steam are the whole ceremony; no accompaniment is strictly needed beyond
gratitude.
