---
name: Shoulder Stand
slug: sarvangasana
sanskritName: Sarvāṅgāsana
category: inversion
level: intermediate
summary: The "all-limbs" pose, balance the body vertically on the shoulders and let blood, breath and attention run the other way for a while.
benefits:
  - Traditionally called the "queen of āsanas", held to benefit the whole body
  - Drains the legs, calms the nerves and quiets the mind
  - Gently stimulates the thyroid and throat in tradition
  - Builds shoulder strength and body-wide coordination
contraindications:
  - Neck injuries, hypertension, glaucoma or recent eye surgery; avoid or practise only with a teacher's clearance
  - Menstruation, tradition suggests gentler alternatives
  - Untreated heart conditions, avoid; never turn the head while up
steps:
  - Lie on the back; on an exhale, draw the knees in and rock the hips up, hands supporting the back.
  - Walk the elbows closer together and lift the feet toward the ceiling, body in one line.
  - Weight rests on the shoulders and upper arms, never the fragile neck; a folded blanket under the shoulders is wise.
  - Keep the legs active but the jaw soft; hold 30 seconds to 3 minutes as strength allows.
  - Lower slowly through Halāsana or with bent knees, and rest flat before rolling to the side.
breathingPattern: Slow, even nasal breathing; the throat stays soft, the breath quiet.
associatedDoshas:
  - kapha
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Sarvāṅgāsana, the queen of āsanas
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

Sarvāṅga means "all limbs", the claim is that every part shares in it. Iyengar crowned it the queen
to headstand's king, and the tradition leans on it for everything from worn nerves to heavy legs. Learn
it with blankets and a teacher; then let it be the steadying peak of a practice.
