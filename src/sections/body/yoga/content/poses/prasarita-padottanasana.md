---
name: Wide-Legged Standing Forward Bend
slug: prasarita-padottanasana
sanskritName: Prasārīta Pādottānāsana
category: forward-bend
level: beginner
summary: Feet wide, crown hanging; a grounded, cooling half-inversion that lengthens the spine without demanding hamstring flexibility.
benefits:
  - Stretches the hamstrings, calves, hips and lower back
  - Decompresses the spine and rests the heart
  - Opens the shoulders when the hands clasp behind the back
  - Calms the mind, a cooling posture for heated, busy days
contraindications:
  - Unmedicated high blood pressure, glaucoma or eye surgery; keep the torso horizontal or come up slowly
  - Hamstring tears, bend the knees generously
  - Lower-back disc issues, stay halfway with hands on a chair
steps:
  - Step the feet wide (about one leg's length), toes turned slightly in, outer edges of the feet parallel.
  - Hands on hips, inhale and lift the chest; exhale and hinge forward from the hips with a long spine.
  - Lower the hands to the floor, blocks, or clasp them behind the back; let the head hang.
  - Keep the weight even across both feet and the knees softly unlocked.
  - Hold 5–10 breaths; to come up, hands to hips and rise on an inhale with a flat back.
breathingPattern: Exhale to fold; long, quiet breaths while inverted; inhale to rise.
associatedDoshas:
  - pitta
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Prasārīta Pādottānāsana
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

Forward bends are the traditional antidote to intensity, haṭha texts frame them as calming, inward
postures. Because the feet stay grounded, this one settles vāta while it cools pitta, making it one of
the most broadly useful folds to linger in.
