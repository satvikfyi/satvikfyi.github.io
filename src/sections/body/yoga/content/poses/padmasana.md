---
name: Lotus Pose
slug: padmasana
sanskritName: Padmāsana
category: meditative
level: advanced
summary: The famed lotus seat, each foot cradled on the opposite thigh; the supreme meditation posture of the haṭha texts, to be earned not forced.
benefits:
  - Locks the body into a stable, self-supporting seat for long meditation
  - Opens the hips, ankles and knees deeply
  - Traditionally said to steady the breath and direct energy inward
  - Carries centuries of association with contemplative stability
contraindications:
  - Knee, ankle or hip injuries; practise half lotus or Sukhāsana instead
  - Stiff hips, never force the knees down; earn the pose over months
  - This pose has a real injury risk when forced, respect its advanced rating
steps:
  - Sit tall; cradle the right foot and place it high on the left thigh, sole up.
  - Bring the left foot onto the right thigh the same way, both knees resting toward the floor.
  - Rest the hands on the knees, palms up in jñāna mudrā, or palms down for grounding.
  - Lengthen the spine; let the body become still, steady and light.
  - Sit for breathing or meditation, then release the legs and repeat with the opposite foot first.
breathingPattern: Slow, subtle nasal breathing; the pose is traditionally paired with praṇāyāma and meditation.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 1, verses 46–49; placing the feet on the thighs, the lotus that destroys disease
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Padmāsana
---

The Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā calls the lotus "the destroyer of all diseases" for the yogi who can hold it,
 but the tradition is equally clear that it is approached gradually, over years. Half lotus, then a
supported cross-leg, carry the same steadiness while the hips open at their own honest pace.
