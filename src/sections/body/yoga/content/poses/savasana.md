name: Corpse Pose
slug: savasana
sanskritName: Śavāsana
category: restful
level: beginner
summary: Lie flat, fully awake and fully still; the hardest pose in the book and the one every practice ends with; conscious, deliberate rest.
benefits:
  - Releases tension systematically from every part of the body
  - Settles the nervous system, tridoshic by reputation, the great balancer
  - Prepares the mind for meditation and deepens the integration of practice
  - Teaches the difference between relaxation and collapse
contraindications:
  - Late pregnancy, rest on the left side instead
  - Back discomfort, place a cushion under the knees or a rolled blanket under the thighs
  - Low mood or dissociation, keep the eyes softly open until settled
steps:
  - Lie on the back, legs falling naturally apart, arms beside the body, palms up.
  - Let the shoulder blades settle evenly; tuck the chin slightly to lengthen the neck.
  - Close the eyes (or soften the gaze); take one deliberate, sighing exhale.
  - Scan from feet to face, releasing each part; then let the breath breathe itself.
  - Rest 5–15 minutes; to finish, wiggle the fingers and toes, roll to the right side, and rise slowly.
breathingPattern: No technique at all, natural breath, watched but unguided; that is the whole practice.

Śavāsana looks like sleep and asks for its opposite: attention without effort. The Haṭha Yoga
Pradīpikā lists it almost casually among the four chief sitting-and-lying poses, then credits it with
destroying fatigue, every teacher since has ended class with it for the same reason. For every dosha,
the instruction is identical: stay, and let go a little more.
