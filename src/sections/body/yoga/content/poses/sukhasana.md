name: Easy Pose
slug: sukhasana
sanskritName: Sukhāsana
category: meditative
level: beginner
summary: Simple cross-legged sitting, sukhāsana, "the pose of ease"; the doorway seat for breathing practices and meditation.
benefits:
  - Opens the hips and lengthens the spine in a relaxed, sustainable way
  - Provides the stable-yet-easy base classical texts require for prāṇāyāma
  - Grounds scattered energy and settles the breath
  - Adapts to nearly every body with a blanket or wall nearby
contraindications:
  - Knee injuries, support the knees on cushions or sit on a chair with feet flat
  - Lower-back weakness, sit on a folded blanket so the knees fall below the hips
  - Numbness or pins-and-needles, change the cross of the legs halfway through
steps:
  - Sit on a folded blanket; cross the shins so each foot rests beneath the opposite knee.
  - Let the knees fall comfortably wide; hands rest on the knees or in the lap.
  - Stack the shoulders over the hips and the crown over the pelvis; chin level.
  - Breathe slowly through the nose, feeling the sit bones and the tall spine.
  - Sit for a few minutes; swap the cross of the legs for balance.
breathingPattern: Natural breath becoming slower and quieter, a seat for prāṇāyāma, not a shape to hold under strain.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata

Sukha, ease; is half of Patañjali's formula for every posture (sthira-sukham āsanam, Yoga Sūtra
2.46). When lotus is far away, this humble seat is not a compromise but the practice itself: sit, be
steady, be at ease. Vāta in particular is soothed by a few unhurried minutes here each day.
