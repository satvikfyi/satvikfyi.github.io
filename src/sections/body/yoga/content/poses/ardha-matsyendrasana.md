---
name: Half Lord of the Fishes Pose
slug: ardha-matsyendrasana
sanskritName: Ardha Matsyendrāsana
category: twist
level: intermediate
summary: The classic seated spiral, named for the teacher Matsyendranāth; wringing the spine gently while feeding the digestive fire.
benefits:
  - Rotates and hydrates the whole spine
  - Stimulates the abdominal organs, liver and digestion
  - Opens the outer hip and glutes
  - Traditionally credited with kindling agni and awakening kuṇḍalinī in the haṭha lore
contraindications:
  - Recent spine injury or surgery, wait for recovery and a teacher's guidance
  - Pregnancy, practise an open, gentle seated twist instead
  - Severe disc problems, keep the twist small and both sit bones grounded
steps:
  - Sit with the legs extended; bend the right knee and step the right foot outside the left thigh.
  - Bend the left knee and bring the left heel toward the right outer hip (or keep the left leg extended).
  - Inhale and lengthen the spine; exhale and rotate toward the right, right hand behind for support.
  - Hug the right knee with the left arm, or hook the left elbow outside the knee.
  - Turn from the belly upward, neck last; hold 5–8 breaths.
  - Release on an exhale and repeat twisting to the left.
breathingPattern: Inhale to lengthen, exhale to rotate; grow tall first, twist second.
associatedDoshas:
  - kapha
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 1, verses 26–27; the teaching seat of Matsyendra among the chief āsanas
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Ardha Matsyendrāsana I
---

Matsyendranāth, the fish-bodied sage of tradition, is said to have overheard Śiva teaching the deepest
yoga at the bottom of the ocean. The pose named for his seat is the haṭha canon's great twist, 
traditionally allied with strong digestion and an awakened spine, friendly to kapha and gently
rearranging for vāta.
