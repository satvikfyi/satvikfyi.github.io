---
name: Cobra Pose
slug: bhujangasana
sanskritName: Bhujaṅgāsana
category: backbend
level: beginner
summary: Lie prone and lift the chest like a rising serpent, a moderate, invigorating backbend that opens the heart and fires the digestion.
benefits:
  - Strengthens the spinal muscles and opens the chest and shoulders
  - Stimulates the abdominal organs and appetite
  - Invigorates the body, a morning posture for dull, heavy mornings
  - May ease mild sciatica and menstrual discomfort in tradition
contraindications:
  - Pregnancy, practised only in modified, gentle form early on, otherwise avoided
  - Wrist or elbow injury, practise a sphinx variation on the forearms
  - Recent abdominal surgery or hernia, wait for medical clearance
  - Severe disc problems, keep the lift small and even along the spine
steps:
  - Lie face down, forehead on the mat, hands under the shoulders, elbows drawn in.
  - Press the tops of the feet and the pubic bone into the floor.
  - Inhale and peel the chest up, leading with the sternum; a low cobra is plenty.
  - Draw the shoulders back and down; keep a slight bend in the elbows and the neck long.
  - Hold 3–5 breaths, then lower on an exhale; rest the forehead and repeat 2–3 times.
breathingPattern: Inhale to rise, exhale to lower; breathe evenly across the open chest while holding.

The serpent rising with hood spread is one of haṭha yoga's oldest images, kuṇḍalinī herself is
pictured as a cobra. As a chest-opening, heat-making backbend, Bhujaṅgāsana is natural medicine for
kapha's heaviness; pitta bodies should keep the lift modest and the breath generous.
