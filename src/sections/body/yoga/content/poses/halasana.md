---
name: Plough Pose
slug: halasana
sanskritName: Halāsana
category: inversion
level: intermediate
summary: From shoulder stand, lower the feet overhead to the floor behind; the plough that turns the body's soil, a deep quiet stretch.
benefits:
  - Stretches the whole spine, shoulders and hamstrings profoundly
  - Calms the nervous system, traditionally quieting for restlessness and insomnia
  - Stimulates the abdominal organs and thyroid in tradition
  - Encourages inward-turning attention (pratyāhāra practice)
contraindications:
  - Neck injury, hypertension, glaucoma or recent eye surgery; avoid
  - Pregnancy outside a trained practice, avoid
  - Asthma or a cold, keep the hold short so breathing stays easy
  - Never force the feet to the floor, support them on a block or chair seat
steps:
  - From Sarvāṅgāsana, support the back with the hands and lower the feet overhead, one at a time.
  - Rest the toes on the floor, a block, or a chair seat behind the head.
  - Keep the legs extended, the torso as vertical as comfort allows, weight on the shoulders.
  - Toes curled under or feet relaxed; arms stay supporting the back or extend on the floor.
  - Hold 30 seconds to 2 minutes; rise back through shoulder stand with control, then lower to rest.
breathingPattern: Slow, quiet abdominal breathing; the folded torso breathes shallow by design; never strain.
associatedDoshas:
  - kapha
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Halāsana
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

The plough turns the earth over so new things can grow; the pose turns the body over so the mind can
run quiet. Following shoulder stand, it deepens the drainage and the hush. Supported feet and a modest
hold keep the neck safe while the nerves settle, a boon for tired vāta and stagnant kapha alike.
