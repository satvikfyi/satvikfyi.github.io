---
name: Wind-Relieving Pose
slug: pavanamuktasana
sanskritName: Pāvanamuktāsana
category: supine
level: beginner
summary: Hug the knees to the chest and rock gently, the folk remedy pose of every Indian household, releasing wind and easing the low back.
benefits:
  - Releases trapped gas and eases bloating (pāvana = wind, mukta = released)
  - Gently massages the abdominal organs and kindles digestion
  - Softens the lower back and releases the hip flexors
  - Calms the nervous system when rocked side to side
contraindications:
  - Recent abdominal surgery or hernia, avoid the compression until cleared
  - Pregnancy, the compression on the belly makes it unsuitable
  - Knee injury, hold behind the thighs rather than over the shins
steps:
  - Lie on the back, legs extended; exhale and draw the right knee toward the chest.
  - Clasp the hands over the shin or behind the thigh; keep the left leg long and relaxed.
  - Hold 5–8 breaths, feeling the right hip and low back soften.
  - Lower the right leg and repeat with the left.
  - Finally hug both knees, rock gently side to side, and rest.
breathingPattern: Exhale to draw the knees in; slow, quiet breathing while holding.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
    reference: the wind-relieving series (pawanmuktasana)
  - title: Ayurvedic Cooking for Self-Healing
    author: Vasant Lad & Usha Lad
---

The Sanskrit name states the claim plainly: this is wind-release, vāta's own house remedy. Grandmothers
prescribe it for a bloated stomach; the haṭha tradition folds it into the gentle pawanmuktasana series
that opens every practice. A minute here after a long day is a small kindness to the gut.
