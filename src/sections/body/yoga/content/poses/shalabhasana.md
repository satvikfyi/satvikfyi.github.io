---
name: Locust Pose
slug: shalabhasana
sanskritName: Śalabhāsana
category: backbend
level: beginner
summary: Lie prone and lift everything, chest, arms and legs; like a locust in flight; a quiet strength-builder for the whole back line.
benefits:
  - Strengthens the entire back body, glutes and hamstrings
  - Opens the chest and improves posture
  - Stimulates digestion and the abdominal organs
  - Builds heat and counters long hours of sitting
contraindications:
  - Pregnancy, this prone lift is not practised after the first trimester
  - Recent back injury, lift lower and for fewer breaths, or rest the hands
  - Headache, keep the neck in line and the gaze down
steps:
  - Lie face down, arms alongside the body, palms down, chin resting lightly on the mat.
  - Exhale to firm the belly; press the hips and pubic bone down into the floor.
  - Inhale and lift the chest, arms and legs away from the floor; reach back through the fingers and toes.
  - Keep the neck long, gaze slightly forward and down; hold 3–5 breaths.
  - Exhale and lower with control; rest, then repeat once or twice.
breathingPattern: Inhale to lift, breathe evenly while holding, exhale to lower.
associatedDoshas:
  - kapha
sources:
  - title: Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā
    author: Gheraṇḍa
    reference: chapter 2, the locust among the classical āsanas
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Śalabhāsana
---

Śalabha, the locust, flies low and works hard. The pose looks modest and demands much, which is why
teachers love it for kapha constitutions and desk-weary spines: it restores the strength that slouching
borrows, without the drama of deeper backbends.
