---
name: Child's Pose
slug: balasana
sanskritName: Bālāsana
category: restful
level: beginner
summary: Fold back over the heels with the forehead down, the child's resting pose, a cool dark cave to return to between every effort.
benefits:
  - Releases the back, shoulders and hips gently
  - Calms the mind and cools intensity, the classic pause pose
  - Softens the breath into the back of the ribs
  - Eases fatigue between stronger poses or at the end of a day
contraindications:
  - Knee injury or recent knee surgery, place a cushion between the heels and hips or avoid
  - Pregnancy, take the knees wide so the belly is free
  - Diarrhoea or an upset stomach, avoid the deep fold
  - Ankle discomfort, place a rolled towel under the ankles
steps:
  - Kneel with the big toes touching and the knees together or wide.
  - Exhale and fold the torso forward between or over the thighs; sit the hips toward the heels.
  - Rest the forehead on the mat, arms extended forward or alongside the body.
  - Let every exhale release the back a little more; breathe into the back ribs.
  - Stay 8–20 breaths, longer whenever needed; rise slowly on an inhale.
breathingPattern: Exhale to fold; soft breathing felt in the back of the ribcage.
associatedDoshas:
  - vata
  - pitta

Every practice needs a home to return to, and this is the one; forehead down, world distant, breath
slow. For pitta it is a cooling cave after heat; for vāta a shelter after wobble. Teachers repeat it
like a mantra: rest is not the absence of practice; sometimes it is the practice.
