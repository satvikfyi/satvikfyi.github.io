---
name: Tree Pose
slug: vrikshasana
sanskritName: Vṛkṣāsana
category: balance
level: beginner
summary: Root through one foot and grow the other into the standing thigh, the classical balance that steadies body and wandering mind alike.
benefits:
  - Strengthens the standing leg, ankles and calves
  - Improves balance and proprioception
  - Opens the hips and inner thighs
  - Cultivates one-pointed focus (dhāraṇā practice in miniature)
contraindications:
  - Balance difficulties or inner-ear issues, practise near a wall or with toes kept on the floor
  - Knee injury on the standing side, keep a micro-bend and use support
  - Headache or insomnia days, keep the gaze low and hold briefly
steps:
  - Stand in Tāḍāsana; shift the weight into the left foot, spreading the toes.
  - Place the sole of the right foot against the inner left thigh or calf, never against the knee joint.
  - Press the foot and the thigh gently toward each other; hips stay level and facing forward.
  - Bring the palms together at the chest, or raise the arms overhead like branches.
  - Soften the gaze onto one still point and breathe for 4–8 breaths.
  - Release with control and repeat standing on the right foot.
breathingPattern: Smooth, unhurried nasal breathing; let the breath (not the eyes) set the pace.

  - vata

Vṛkṣa means tree, and the image is the instruction: roots below, trunk steady, branches easy. For
vata's airy, scattered energy, slow balancing poses are traditional medicine; each wobble is simply
another breath to begin again.
