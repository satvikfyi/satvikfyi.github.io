---
name: Fish Pose
slug: matsyasana
sanskritName: Matsyāsana
category: backbend
level: intermediate
summary: Lie back and arch the chest skyward, cradled on the forearms; the fish that swam Matsya's waters, opener of the heart and throat.
benefits:
  - Deeply opens the chest, throat and front shoulders
  - Stretches the hip flexors and the front body
  - Traditionally a counter-pose to shoulder stand, bathing the throat and thyroid
  - Breathes new depth into a shallow, slouched breath
contraindications:
  - Neck injury or neck sensitivity, support the head on a block or keep it lifted
  - High blood pressure or migraine, practise a gentle, propped variation
  - Lower-back pain, keep the legs extended and the lift moderate
steps:
  - Lie on the back, legs extended or knees bent, arms alongside the body.
  - Press the forearms down and lift the chest; slide onto the crown or the back of the head (never the throat).
  - Keep the weight shared between forearms and head, never resting fully on the neck.
  - Broaden the collarbones; breathe into the open chest for 5–8 breaths.
  - Exhale, lower the chest first, then ease the head down; rest and integrate.
breathingPattern: Lift on an exhale; breathe wide and full into the open ribs while holding.
associatedDoshas:
  - kapha
sources:
  - title: Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā
    author: Gheraṇḍa
    reference: chapter 2, the fish, "destroyer of diseases", floating on water
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Matsyāsana
---

Matsya is the fish avatar, the divine fish that saved the Vedas from the flood. The Gheraṇḍa
Saṃhitā praises the pose named for it. As the traditional counter-pose to Sarvāṅgāsana it re-opens
the neck; alone, it is a chest-opener that shakes off kapha's damp and lets the breath run deep.
