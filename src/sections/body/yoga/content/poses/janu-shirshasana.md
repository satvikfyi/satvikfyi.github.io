---
name: Head-to-Knee Pose
slug: janu-shirshasana
sanskritName: Jānu Śīrṣāsana
category: forward-bend
level: beginner
summary: Fold over one extended leg while the other rests bent open, an asymmetric, gentle fold that meets a tight back with kindness.
benefits:
  - Stretches the hamstrings, calves and one side of the back
  - Opens the hip and groin of the bent leg
  - Tones the abdominal organs on the folding side
  - Encourages quiet, inward attention
contraindications:
  - Knee injury on the bent side, support the knee with a cushion or keep it raised
  - Lower-back pain, fold less deeply, keeping the spine long
  - Pregnancy, take a wide stance so the belly is never compressed
steps:
  - Sit with both legs extended; bend the right knee and open it to the right, sole against the inner left thigh.
  - Square the torso over the left leg; inhale and raise the arms long.
  - Exhale and fold over the extended leg, chest leading; hold the foot, shin or a strap.
  - Keep the shoulders even and the neck soft; settle with each exhale for 6–12 breaths.
  - Inhale to rise; repeat with the left knee bent.
breathingPattern: Exhale to fold; slow belly breaths while holding; inhale to rise.
associatedDoshas:
  - pitta
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Jānu Śīrṣāsana
  - title: Asana Pranayama Mudra Bandha
    author: Swami Satyananda Saraswati
---

Because each side folds separately, Jānu Śīrṣāsana reveals, and forgives, the body's asymmetries.
Tradition reads the pose as cooling and grounding: the bent knee opens the hip where vāta gathers
tension, while the unhurried fold pours water on pitta's flame.
