---
name: Seated Forward Bend
slug: paschimottanasana
sanskritName: Paścimottānāsana
category: forward-bend
level: beginner
summary: The classical seated fold over the extended legs, praised in the haṭha texts for turning the mind inward and waking the digestive fire.
benefits:
  - Stretches the entire back line, heels, hamstrings, hips and spine
  - Tones the abdominal organs and kindles agni (digestive fire)
  - Calms the nervous system and prepares the mind for meditation
  - Traditionally said to reduce appetite and restlessness when held long
contraindications:
  - Disc problems or acute lower-back pain, keep the spine long and fold less far
  - Hamstring injury, bend the knees or sit on a folded blanket
  - Asthma felt on the exhale, rise slightly and breathe freely before easing down again
steps:
  - Sit with the legs extended, feet together, spine tall (sit on a folded blanket if the lower back rounds).
  - Inhale and raise the arms, lengthening the whole spine upward.
  - Exhale and hinge forward from the hips, chest leading toward the toes.
  - Hold the feet, ankles or shins, or loop a strap around the feet, shoulders soft.
  - With each exhale settle a little further, without pulling or rounding; hold 8–15 breaths.
  - Inhale to rise slowly back to tall sitting.
breathingPattern: Exhale to deepen; breathe into the back of the body while holding; never hold the breath.
associatedDoshas:
  - pitta
  - vata
sources:
  - title: Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā
    author: Svātmārāma
    reference: chapter 1, verses 28–29; stretching the back, praised among the chief āsanas
  - title: Light on Yoga
    author: B. K. S. Iyengar
    reference: Paścimottānāsana
---

Paścima means "the west", the back of the body, where the sun sets. The Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā places
this fold among its select āsanas and credits it with kindling the digestive fire and stilling the
restless mind. It is the classic cooling fold: pitta softens in it, and vāta grounds when it is held
with patience rather than pulled.
