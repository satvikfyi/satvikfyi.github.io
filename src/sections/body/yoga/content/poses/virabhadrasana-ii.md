---
name: Warrior II
slug: virabhadrasana-ii
sanskritName: Vīrabhadrāsana II
category: standing
level: beginner
summary: A wide, steady stance with arms stretched horizon-wide; strength, stamina and calm focus in one classical shape.
benefits:
  - Strengthens and tones the legs, ankles and arms
  - Opens the hips, groin and shoulders
  - Builds stamina and circulation
  - Develops concentration and a steady, courageous gaze (dṛṣṭi)
contraindications:
  - Knee or hip injuries, shorten the stance and keep the front knee shallow
  - Neck strain, keep the head centred instead of turning it
  - High blood pressure, hold for fewer breaths and keep the arms lower
steps:
  - Step the feet wide (about one leg's length); turn the right foot out 90° and the left toes slightly in.
  - Inhale and raise the arms to shoulder height, palms down, reaching from fingertip to fingertip.
  - Exhale and bend the right knee over the right ankle, thigh aiming toward parallel with the floor.
  - Keep the back leg strong and pressing into the outer edge of the back foot; hips stay square to the long edge of the mat.
  - Turn the gaze softly over the front hand and hold 3–6 breaths.
  - Inhale to straighten the leg; repeat on the second side.
breathingPattern: Inhale to lift the arms; exhale to bend the front knee; breathe evenly and deeply while holding.

Named for Vīrabhadra, the fierce warrior born of Śiva's grief in the Purāṇic telling, the warrior
poses belong to the modern standing-pose canon shaped by Krishnamacharya's lineage. The paradox is the
teaching: the shape is martial, but the face stays soft.
