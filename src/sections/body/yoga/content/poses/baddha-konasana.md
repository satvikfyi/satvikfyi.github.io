---
name: Bound Angle Pose
slug: baddha-konasana
sanskritName: Baddha Koṇāsana
category: seated
level: beginner
summary: Soles together, knees wide open; the butterfly seat that quietly opens the deepest hips and steadies the breath.
benefits:
  - Opens the hips, inner thighs and groin
  - Improves mobility for seated meditation postures
  - Stimulates the abdominal organs and bladder
  - Traditionally easing for menstrual discomfort and tired feet
contraindications:
  - Groin or knee injuries, support the knees on cushions or blocks
  - Sciatica on the sitting bones, sit on a folded blanket to tilt the pelvis upright
  - Lower-back strain, keep the spine long and avoid collapsing the chest
steps:
  - Sit tall; bend the knees and bring the soles of the feet together, heels a comfortable distance from the body.
  - Hold the feet like a book opening; let the knees fall toward the floor without forcing.
  - Lengthen the spine; sit on a blanket if the lower back rounds backward.
  - Stay upright for 8–12 breaths, or fold forward gently with a long spine.
  - To release, draw the knees together and extend the legs.
breathingPattern: Even, unhurried breathing; if folding, exhale to settle forward.

Also called the cobbler's pose after the seated posture of village shoemakers, Baddha Koṇāsana is a
preparation for Padmāsana and a practice in its own right. Where vāta hardens the hips and pitta
shortens the breath, this seat asks only for time; gravity does the opening.
