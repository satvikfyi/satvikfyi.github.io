---
name: Downward Facing Dog Pose
slug: adho-mukha-svanasana
sanskritName: Adho Mukha Śvānāsana
category: forward-bend
level: beginner
summary: The iconic inverted V, hands and feet rooted, hips high; one pose that stretches, strengthens and rests the heart all at once.
benefits:
  - Stretches the hamstrings, calves, shoulders and spine
  - Strengthens the arms, wrists and core
  - Acts as a mild inversion, calming the head and resting the heart
  - Energises the body while quietening the mind
contraindications:
  - Wrist injuries, practise on forearms or with hands on a chair seat
  - Late pregnancy, keep the torso higher and the stance wider
  - Unmedicated high blood pressure, glaucoma or recent eye surgery; keep the head level with or above the heart
steps:
  - From hands and knees, tuck the toes and lift the hips up and back on an exhale.
  - Press the palms evenly into the floor, fingers spread, outer arms rolling in.
  - Let the head hang heavy between the arms; lengthen from wrists to hips.
  - Reach the heels toward (not necessarily to) the floor; knees can stay softly bent.
  - Hold 5–10 breaths, then bend the knees and lower back to hands and knees or fold forward.
breathingPattern: Exhale into the shape; breathe deeply and evenly while holding, the pose is a resting inversion, not a strain.

The dog stretches after its nap, the name is a household image from the haṭha revival of the 20th
century, though inverted shapes appear in older texts under other names. Half dog (hands on a wall or
chair) gives the same calming, head-below-heart effect with far less load on the wrists.
